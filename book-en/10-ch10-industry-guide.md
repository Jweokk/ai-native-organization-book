# Chapter 10 Industry Guide: AI-Native Transformation Across Ten Industries

The first nine chapters set out the general principles of the AI-native organization; this chapter brings them down to the industry level. The choice of ten industries follows the triage framework from Chapter 3 — **the ratio of information bottlenecks to physical bottlenecks determines how much organizational value AI can unlock**: where the bottleneck lies in information processing (finance, professional services, media), AI's speedup is structural; where it lies in the physical world (manufacturing, energy), AI only addresses the "documentation and administrative layer surrounding production," and expectations must be discounted accordingly.

The ten industries are ordered from the highest to the lowest share of information bottleneck. Each industry section contains five parts: industry profile and bottleneck triage, transformation paths and typical practices, benchmark cases, failure warnings, and organizational patterns and consulting takeaways — together, the five parts form a condensed map of that industry's AI-native transformation. **How to read this chapter**: if your industry is not among the ten, first find the industry whose information-bottleneck ratio is closest to yours, then apply the four-phase roadmap from Chapter 8.

## 10.1 Professional Services & Legal

### 10.1.1 Industry Profile and Bottleneck Triage

Professional services (law, audit, consulting) have the highest share of "information bottleneck" of any industry in this book's framework: roughly nine-tenths of the work is the acquisition, analysis, and production of information, and only one-tenth involves physical delivery — a 90:10 structure that puts the industry almost entirely within range of generative AI. For the past two decades, competitiveness in this industry has rested on "headcount leverage": hiring large numbers of junior staff to handle structured work such as research, summarization, and first drafts, while senior experts make the judgments at the top of the pyramid. AI compresses precisely the base of that pyramid.

The industry therefore faces a sharp paradox. The essence of the AI shock is the productization of knowledge work — turning judgment processes once sold by the hour into software assets that are reproducible, deployable, and priceable. Yet the industry's very foundation is client trust and professional responsibility. Lawyers, auditors, and consultants bear unlimited liability for their output, while large language models carry an inherent risk of hallucination. Trust, responsibility, and hallucination constitute the core contradiction of the industry's AI-native transformation: the more aggressively you pursue efficiency, the higher the cost of an error. Understanding this is a prerequisite for understanding every success and every failure that follows.

### 10.1.2 Transformation Paths and Typical Practices

**Law firms: from watching to deploying.** Thomson Reuters' 2026 AI in Professional Services Report found that 41% of law firms and 47% of corporate legal departments already use GenAI within their teams, up from 28% and 23% in 2025 [third-party verified]; by task, 77% use it for document review, 74% for legal research, 74% for summarization, and 59% for drafting. But the ABA's 2025 survey revealed a structural mismatch: individual use has surged, yet firm-wide adoption stands at only 39% among firms with 51 or more lawyers and roughly 20% among firms with fewer than 50 [third-party verified]. Another data point is colder still — "28% usage, but only about 15% full deployment" [third-party verified]. Between surface-level adoption and organizational capability lie three hurdles: policy, data security, and training. China's pace is entirely different: 88% of legal professionals already use AI tools [third-party verified], and the Ministry of Justice positions AI as a "digital-intelligence engine," with the framing being "efficiency first" rather than "ethics constraints first" [third-party verified].

**Legal AI companies: vertical bets with heavy capital.** Harvey is the emblematic case: it made its name in 2023 through an exclusive partnership with Allen & Overy, and its valuation jumped from US$5 billion to US$11 billion in less than a year (March 2026, led by GIC and Sequoia), serving 1,300+ organizations and 100,000+ lawyers, with annualized recurring revenue reaching US$190 million in January 2026 [third-party verified]. Legal-tech funding hit a record US$2.4 billion in 2025 [third-party verified]. Goldman Sachs estimates that roughly 17% of legal jobs are exposed to AI risk — sharply down from the 44% predicted in 2023, because the industry found that AI does more "role-shifting" than "role-eliminating" [third-party verified].

**The Big Four: platformization as a response to "core business disruption."** Deloitte Omnia, PwC GL.ai (in partnership with H2O.ai), EY Helix/EY.ai, and KPMG Ignite embed AI into the core steps of audit evidence collection and risk assessment [third-party verified]; EY Australia already processes about 50% of bank audit confirmations with AI [third-party verified]. Auditing is moving from "sample checking" toward "full-data analysis" [third-party verified]. Business models are switching in parallel: from billable hours toward multi-year "rebuild and run the enterprise back office" transformation contracts [third-party verified]. The talent structure is the most visible indicator — in 2025, the Big Four hired more people for AI-related roles than for traditional audit roles, with audit positions accounting for less than 3% of job postings [third-party verified].

**Consultancies: shrinking and rebuilding in parallel.** McKinsey plans to cut up to 10% of staff in non-client-facing functions, bringing headcount from a peak of 45,000+ during the expansion era back to about 40,000 [third-party verified]; BCG has grown AI-related revenue to about US$3.6 billion, roughly 25% of total revenue [third-party verified]. The industry is splitting into two kinds of businesses: "AI replacers" and "AI enablers."

### 10.1.3 Benchmark Cases

**A&O Shearman: from law firm to software product house.** [official claims] In December 2022 the firm became the first law firm in the world to deploy Harvey enterprise-wide; today 4,000 employees across 43 jurisdictions use the platform. The deployment path was "sandbox first": a small group of lawyers first tried it in an isolated environment and established governance protocols before scaling. In April 2025 the firm launched agentic AI agents together with Harvey, covering four high-value areas — antitrust filing analysis, cybersecurity, fund formation, and loan review — sold to corporate clients and other law firms on a subscription or usage basis, with the firm sharing in the revenue. The head of its AI consulting practice said "these agents complete in minutes work that used to take hours." The firm also built ContractMatrix on the Harvey API, anchoring AI output to existing precedents and policies to reduce hallucination risk, and licenses it to clients as SaaS. Third-party observers add a critical detail: "every AI output is subject to human audit" [third-party verified]. This is the most complete sample of "productizing professional knowledge" — lawyers' knowledge becomes part of the technology stack, and the firm shifts from selling hours to selling software.

**Accenture: full-workforce AI fluency plus outcome-based pricing.** [third-party verified] When ChatGPT launched, Accenture had only 30 people working on GenAI and less than US$1 million in related revenue; by FY2025, GenAI revenue reached US$2.7 billion (tripled year over year), full-year revenue was US$69.7 billion (+7%), and the company had accumulated US$5 billion in AI revenue and US$12 billion in AI sales. On the organization side: it has trained 550,000 employees, and from 2026 senior employees' usage of the internal AI platform (AI Refinery) will be factored into promotion reviews — "AI fluency" becomes a hard requirement. On the commercial side: about 60% of work is already fixed-price and increasingly tied to productivity, cycle time, and business KPIs. HFS Research describes this as nonlinear growth — a shift from "services growing with headcount" to "products growing with adoption." Using productization to escape headcount leverage makes Accenture the most complete financial sample of AI-native reinvention in professional services.

**The scale sample of firm-wide law firm deployment.** Norton Rose reports that 80% of its lawyers have adopted legal-specific AI [third-party verified]; Macfarlanes built its own Amplify platform, combining Harvey's capabilities with the firm's institutional knowledge, with internal adoption above 80% [third-party verified]; a Bloomberg Law survey found that in 2025, every firm with 500+ lawyers that disclosed its technology details was using legal-specific AI tools [third-party verified]. The common trait of successful scalers: not buying the most tools, but institutionalizing "human-in-the-loop auditing."

### 10.1.4 Failure Warnings

**Wadsworth v. Walmart: the self-built AI crash.** [third-party verified] Eight of the nine case citations generated by the firm's self-built AI platform MX2.law did not exist; three Morgan & Morgan lawyers were fined and had their temporary licenses revoked. By mid-2026, courts worldwide had handled 1,598 sanctions cases involving AI-fabricated citations, with penalties escalating from fines to suspension and even disbarment; the highest single fine was about US$109,700. Stanford's RegLab research is more unsettling: even professional legal AI tools hallucinate at rates of 17%–34% [third-party verified]. Courts have been highly consistent: a lawyer's duty to verify cannot be delegated to tools, subordinates, or vendors.

**DoNotPay: the collapse of consumer-grade legal AI.** [third-party verified] Founded in 2015 and cumulatively funded to about US$25 million, the "world's first robot lawyer" effectively collapsed in 2024. The failure chain is clear: consumer-grade AI cannot guarantee accurate legal output → this triggers "unauthorized practice of law" risk → the FTC finalized its order in February 2025, finding deceptive advertising and imposing monetary relief. The founder had declared he would "replace the US$200 billion legal industry with AI"; in the end, it was the regulator that drew the boundary. The lesson: the entry barrier for legal services is "trustworthy and compliant," and the consumer-internet model of rapid iteration fundamentally conflicts with professional accountability.

**Deloitte Australia: having a platform is not the same as having governance.** [third-party verified] A report delivered by Deloitte Australia to the government — worth A$440,000 (about US$290,000) — was "riddled with AI-generated errors"; the firm eventually admitted to using AI and refunded the fee. Its self-built chatbot AskDeloitte had been used to draft client reports, and the errors stemmed from a lack of adequate human verification. After the incident, EY, KPMG, PwC, and BCG raced to claim they had rigorous review processes. A single AI failure can erode the most core asset of a professional institution — "the output can be trusted."

### 10.1.5 Organizational Patterns and Consulting Takeaways

Four patterns are worth borrowing for any professional-services organization.

First, **guardrails for hallucination and liability must be designed up front**. ABA Formal Opinion 512 establishes the baseline framework: the duty of competence (understand the tool's capabilities and limitations; AI output must be independently verified), the duty of confidentiality (assess leakage risk before inputting client data), candor toward the tribunal (citations must be verified), and the duty of supervision (management must establish AI policies and train everyone) [third-party verified]. On the ground this takes the form of institutionalized "human-in-the-loop": mandatory human audit of AI output and a routinized citation-verification process. Guardrails are not the opposite of efficiency; they are the precondition for scaling AI — the Deloitte Australia crash proves that the larger the platform without guardrails, the greater the risk.

Second, **productizing knowledge assets is the second growth curve**. A&O's ContractMatrix, Macfarlanes' Amplify, and Chinese firm PowerLaw AI's (Chinese: 幂律智能) hybrid architecture of "AI models + sedimented lawyer rules" all point down the same path: move experts' judgment rules out of people's heads and into systems, then sell them outward. But the threshold is real — Pacgate Law Firm's (Chinese: 百宸律师事务所) summary is the soberest: data assets (whether structured, trainable data can be accumulated), organizational process (a closed-loop human-verification loop), compliance and ethics, and an AI engineering team that understands law — all four are indispensable [third-party verified]. Most institutions will stay at "using AI tools"; only a few will become "AI law firms."

Third, **the talent structure shifts from a pyramid to "senior experts + AI leverage."** The Big Four hire more AI staff than accountants, BCG adds only AI and technology specialists rather than generalist consultants, Accenture writes AI fluency into promotion criteria — the compression of junior repetitive roles is a certain trend, but "role-shifting" outweighs "role-eliminating": analytical judgment, client relationships, and accountability become more valuable. In the AI era, the scarce commodity is "people who can take responsibility for AI output."

Fourth, **billing must shift from billable hours to value-based pricing**. This is the industry's deepest contradiction: under ABA 512, work that AI completes in 15 minutes can only be billed for 15 minutes — effectively penalizing efficiency [third-party verified]; meanwhile Accenture's 60% fixed-price work, the Big Four's move toward multi-year outcome contracts, and A&O's subscription software sales all point in the same direction: pay for outcomes and platforms, not for hours. Whoever makes the switch first wins pricing power; those who delay are locked into the paradox of "working faster and earning less."

## 10.2 Media, Content & Creative Industries

### 10.2.1 Industry Profile and Bottleneck Triage

Media, content, and creative industries have one of the highest information-bottleneck shares of any industry, with information processing weighing in at roughly 90:10 of the value chain. Generative AI's impact here is not "icing on the cake" — it strikes directly at the core factor of production. The industry's essence is a "trust business": content institutions sell authenticity, judgment, and emotional resonance, and generative AI simultaneously dismantles and amplifies all three — it drives the cost of producing content toward zero, and the cost of producing fake content toward zero as well.

The industry faces three overlapping structural contradictions. First, the content-copyright game: rights holders and AI companies are lining up on two tracks — "licensing partnerships" versus "litigation confrontation." Second, trust and authenticity: tests by the BBC and the European Broadcasting Union of four major AI assistants found that nearly half of responses contained significant errors and 31% involved misquotation; Reuters Institute research shows that only about 7% of online news consumers use AI assistants to get news, yet trust in AI news among people under 35 is already close to 50% — younger audiences are becoming "AI natives," and institutional media's authoritative narrative has not yet found a new anchor. Third, the flood of AI content farms: NewsGuard has identified 3,749 AI content farms; one suspect generated 4,000–7,000 fake news articles in a single day at peak, and one AI-generated fake news story surpassed 500 million reads. When content supply becomes infinite, what becomes scarce is no longer content but trustworthy content.

### 10.2.2 Transformation Paths and Typical Practices

**Newsroom AI** started earliest and is the most institutionalized. Xinhua built its intelligent editorial department in 2019, using "Media Brain" plus AI synthetic anchors to cover 30-plus content categories, establishing the paradigm of "AI mass production, human review gatekeeping." People's Daily's AI editorial department has iterated to version 6.0, using the "Zhimei Engine" (Chinese: 智媒引擎) to deliver generative text-to-image and image-to-video production; its short film The Protagonist (Chinese: 《主角》) drew more than 600 million exposures within four days. The Associated Press used Wordsmith to expand earnings-report coverage tenfold, and Reuters' Newsmaker lifted production efficiency tenfold. International media entered organizational reinvention earlier: the FT declared that AI has moved from standalone projects to "infrastructure, pipeline, partner, and competitor"; the BBC set up a dedicated news AI team and requires all employees to hold ChatGPT Enterprise access; the Guardian reached a content-licensing partnership with OpenAI in February 2025.

**Content-platform organizational change** is the most radical organizational experiment in China's internet industry in 2025–2026: Xiaohongshu (Chinese: 小红书) established an AI first-level department, Dots; ByteDance is pushing its organization toward an "intelligence-intensive" transformation with AI capital expenditure exceeding 200 billion yuan, replacing incumbent managers with "people who can use AI"; Kuaishou upgraded its self-developed video model Kling (Chinese: 可灵) to a first-level department with independent financing. The common choice of all three: "AI is promoted to an organizational unit" rather than "AI is scattered around as a tool."

**AIGC in film, TV, and games** is rewriting the cost structure: AI short dramas compress the localization process into a pipeline and cut costs by 15x; Chinese short-drama apps capture 91% of global revenue, and the overseas micro-drama market surpassed US$4 billion in 2025. In gaming, Tencent's VISVISE, miHoYo's Glossa, and NetEase's "Smart Jianghu" (Chinese: 智能江湖; 400 intelligent NPCs, 30% AI efficiency gains) are all showing their hands. iQiyi built an AI artist library via an "AI Consent Form," turning artists' digital avatars into assets. **AI anchors** have formed an independent industry: by 2024 the segment's scale approached 480 billion yuan, spanning 12 core fields.

### 10.2.3 Benchmark Cases

**Hunan Broadcasting (Chinese: 湖南广电): full-chain self-development plus all-staff training.** Mango TV's paying members surpassed 75.6 million [official claims]; its self-developed Mango LLM is among the first batch of broadcasting-industry large models registered with the Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC) [official claims], has incubated more than 80 agents applied across 30-plus shows, and lifted production efficiency by over 30% [official claims]. What is replicable is the organizational path: in 2024 it launched the generative AI director "Aimang" (Chinese: 爱芒), which served as virtual assistant director on the reality show We Three (Chinese: 《我们仨》), elevating AI from a tool to a "role" within the production team; it launched the "Shanhai AIGC Content Creation Platform" (Chinese: 山海 AIGC 内容创作平台); under the "Daman Plan" (Chinese: 大芒计划), 5,137 micro-dramas had been released cumulatively by 2025, up more than 14x year over year [third-party verified]. In parallel, it ran group-wide AIGC hands-on bootcamps: 800+ people registered and 100 entered intensive practice [third-party verified] — forming a closed loop of "universal training → elite intensive sessions → production-line application," positioning AIGC as "production line and leverage" rather than "demo tool."

**FT: abolishing AI job titles is a watershed in organizational evolution.** The FT announced that from 2026 it will no longer have product director roles whose titles carry "AI" [third-party verified]. The decision looks counterintuitive but is a sign of maturity: once AI is woven into the daily work of all teams, setting up dedicated "AI roles" actually hinders penetration. The FT's strategy has shifted from "build and buy" to "build and partner," and it reached a content-licensing agreement with OpenAI in April 2024; using large models to auto-generate discussion questions lifted its reader comment rate by 3.5% [third-party verified]. This is a sample of "de-specializing AI" — the destination of an AI-native organization is not an AI department but no AI department.

**Kling: from tool to independently financed entity.** Kling AI is the AI video-generation model Kuaishou developed in June 2024: 45 million+ creators worldwide, 200 million videos generated cumulatively, and 20,000+ enterprise customers served [official claims]. In 2025 it was upgraded to a Kuaishou first-level department and commercialized; in July 2026 it completed a nearly US$3 billion independent financing round at a post-money valuation of US$18 billion, with Tencent, Alibaba, and Baidu participating [third-party verified]. The three-step jump — "tool → first-level department → independently financed entity" — is the most complete organizational-change sample of a content platform incubating an AI-native business.

### 10.2.4 Failure Warnings

**The double-edged sword of litigation confrontation: NYT v. OpenAI.** In May 2026, the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York issued its first-instance ruling; the 247-page opinion has been called "the most important judicial document in AI copyright history" [third-party verified]. The case began in December 2023, with claims initially around US$15 billion and later reporting suggesting up to US$22.5 billion; in July 2026 the NYT and others also moved to sanction OpenAI. The lawsuit draws a clear roadmap: the era of "free scraping" without licensing or compensation is over — but the costs and uncertainty of litigation are equally staggering. The copyright game has no single-winner solution, only a combination of "licensing pricing" and "litigation deterrence."

**Trust collapses with a single failure: CNET and Gannett.** Gannett suspended its use of LedeAI after AI-generated sports coverage drew public ridicule for its poor quality; CNET abandoned AI writing after it was found publishing undisclosed AI articles containing factual errors; Sports Illustrated was exposed for an AI-generated fictional author, "Drew Ortiz," and its parent company fired the person in charge. The shared lesson: in an industry built on trust, if you use AI to cut costs at the expense of transparency and quality, the money saved is nowhere near the cost of a brand crisis. The LA Times' AI tool was taken down within a week of launch after defending the Ku Klux Klan; the Chicago Sun-Times' summer reading list included book titles invented by ChatGPT — these cases of "failed guardrails" jointly prove that the quality and value risks of AI content must be backstopped by organizational mechanisms, not by the model's own discretion.

**The ecosystem-level pollution of AI content farms** is the "tragedy of the commons" facing the entire industry: low-quality AI content drowns trustworthy content in volume, the traffic-monetization value chain drives fakers to take risks, and the traditional governance model of "clarify and self-prove" has already failed.

### 10.2.5 Organizational Patterns and Consulting Takeaways

Five patterns can be distilled from the media and content industry's AI-native transformation.

**First, copyright-compliance guardrails must precede efficiency pursuits.** The Guardian and the FT chose licensing; the NYT chose litigation; Suno and Udio eventually settled and paid the record labels; iQiyi's "AI Consent Form" and SAG-AFTRA's writing of AI protections into core contracts (won through the first full shutdown of 160,000 actors in 63 years) all point to the same conclusion: AI transformation in the content industry is first and foremost a renegotiation of the rights structure.

**Second, AI content labeling is a mandatory organizational variable.** On September 1, 2025, the Measures for the Labeling of AI-Generated Synthetic Content, issued by four ministries of the Chinese government, took effect, requiring explicit and implicit labels on AI content — the most systematic AIGC labeling regulatory framework among major economies. Douyin upgraded its labeling features in parallel, throttling, demoting, and even removing unlabeled content. Content institutions must internalize "labeling compliance" as a step in the production process.

**Third, trust assets are a moat that cannot be outsourced.** The BBC/EBU test data draw the red line of "human review required"; "AI mass production, human review gatekeeping" remains the industry benchmark. The industry is moving from "people using tools" toward "agents as autonomous execution units in the production process" — but autonomous execution does not mean no one is accountable: an accountability mechanism must be established in parallel.

**Fourth, the essence of human-AI division of labor is "scale to AI, meaning to humans."** The practice of the world's major wire services shows that AI excels at scale and speed, while humans excel at human care, trust-building, and value judgment. The first question in a transformation consultation is "which steps' scale dividends are worth capturing," not "which jobs will be replaced."

**Fifth, organizational form upgrades with strategy, and policy must precede deployment.** Of the 1,000 AI initiatives across global newsrooms, nearly two-thirds are built in-house, yet only 127 have policies, and only 7% of initiatives directly target monetization [third-party verified] — "fast deployment, slow policy, unresolved monetization" is the industry's common ailment. The implication for consultants: the mark of maturity in content-industry AI-native transformation is not how many AI projects were built, but the disappearance of AI job titles (FT), the labelability of AI content (China's regulation), and human-AI collaboration becoming the default way of working (BBC, Mango).

## 10.3 Financial Services

### 10.3.1 Industry Profile and Bottleneck Triage

Finance is a quintessentially information-intensive industry: a loan decision, a research report, and a claims settlement are all, at bottom, information processing. Its "information bottleneck" to "physical bottleneck" ratio is about 80:20 — which makes it one of the most fertile grounds for large models. Tencent Research Institute's judgment is representative: finance has a high degree of digitalization and high potential value in commercial scenarios, making it one of the best scenarios for AI large-model deployment. McKinsey estimates that generative AI could create US$200 billion to US$340 billion in value for the global banking industry each year — 2.8%–4.7% of the industry's annual revenue.

But fertile soil is not the same as easy plowing. The core contradiction of financial AI transformation is threefold. First, heavy regulation: credit scoring, anti-money laundering, and underwriting are classified as "high risk" under the EU's AI Act, and China's National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA) has also issued guidance on AI application in banking and insurance, requiring full-lifecycle model management and explainability. Second, data sensitivity: customer privacy, transaction data, and model hallucination are directly tied to capital and reputational risk — the warning from Gu Shu, chairman of the Agricultural Bank of China, at the Lujiazui Forum (model black boxes, hallucination, and the uncertainty of autonomous decisions) is industry consensus. Third, strong organizational inertia: IBM research shows that as of 2024 only 8% of banks were systematically developing AI, while 78% still used tactical, fragmented approaches. Finance does not lack AI enthusiasm; it lacks the organizational capability to turn pilots into systems.

### 10.3.2 Transformation Paths and Typical Practices

Banks, insurers, and brokerages have taken three clearly differentiated paths.

Banks are the most stratified. The big state-owned banks pursue a "model matrix plus compute infrastructure" strategy: ICBC's Zhirong (Chinese: 工银智涌) hundred-billion-parameter financial LLM system empowers 20-plus business areas and 200-plus scenarios, with AI digital employees handling the equivalent of 55,000 person-years of work annually. The joint-stock banks focus on deepening scenarios: China Merchants Bank's "AI Xiaozhao" (Chinese: AI 小招) serves over 20 million customers a month and replaced 15.56 million hours of human work with AI over the year; Industrial Bank's agent platform hosts more than 200 agents. The internet banks take a thoroughly AI-native route: WeBank proposes "embedding the process into AI" rather than "embedding AI into the process," and MYbank uses three AI systems — "Shanque" (Chinese: 大山雀), "Dayan" (Chinese: 大雁), and "Bailing" (Chinese: 百灵) — to rebuild micro and SME lending, with the ambition of "giving every small business an AI CFO." In 2025, DeepSeek's open-sourcing triggered a wave of local deployment: 20-plus financial institutions completed private deployments at million-yuan-level cost, and with lightweight open-source financial models such as Fin-R1, the pattern of "financial AI belongs only to big banks" was broken.

The insurance industry is penetrating along the entire value chain — product pricing, marketing and customer acquisition, underwriting, claims, customer service, and operations — with large models upgrading insurance AI from "discriminative tools" to "generative intelligence"; knowledge-intensive steps such as agent training and claims-report writing benefit first. Brokerages have made investment research the main battlefield: large-model adoption in the securities industry reached 26.7%, with cases like Soochow Securities' (Chinese: 东吴证券) multi-agent investment advisor and CSC Financial's (Chinese: 中信建投) industry knowledge base in place, making research the field most likely to first achieve an end-to-end "data-research-investment-risk control" chain.

On organizational change, two routes diverge sharply. JPMorgan Chase chose retraining and flexible transition — employees shift from "makers" to "checkers" (managing AI agents). Standard Chartered, by contrast, announced it will cut more than 15% of corporate-function roles (about 8,000 people) before 2030, with the CEO bluntly saying the roles are being "handed over to machines." DBS runs a dual track of "AI replacement plus AI role creation," committing to hire about 1,000 new AI roles. China's six largest banks saw total headcount grow only slightly — by 1,538 people — at end-2025, while tech talent grew by a net 35,000 to 135,900: the structural blood transfusion is already visible to the naked eye.

### 10.3.3 Benchmark Cases

**WeBank: the most complete sample of an AI-native organization [official claims + third-party verified]**

In 2023 WeBank proposed moving "from digital-native to AI-native," defined as "not AI embedded in the process, but the process embedded in AI." The implementation mechanism has three layers: an AI infrastructure layer (an engineered platform where compute is allocatable, models are pluggable, and outcomes are measurable, supporting private deployment of open-source models such as DeepSeek); an AI application layer (70-plus digital employees and over 800 agents covering more than a thousand business scenarios); and an AI governance layer. By the end of 2025, AI compute had increased 3.5x year over year, daily calls had grown from 41,000 to 2.4 million, and daily token consumption had grown from 200 million to over 5 billion [official claims]. Organizationally, it established a technology product manager role to coordinate technology planning, breaking the old "business raises requirements, technology executes" model; in budgeting, an "Innovate the Bank" allocation funds frontier technology in advance and allows trial and error. This is the most complete sample of AI-native organizational change among domestic Chinese banks.

**JPMorgan Chase: methodology at scale [third-party verified; benefit figures are the CEO's public statements]**

450 production-grade use cases; the AI professional team grew from 1,500 people in 2022 to 2,500+ (+67%). Methodological essentials: use cases must be tied to business KPIs rather than technical metrics; human-machine collaboration design rather than full automation; data governance and model-risk controls first; start with high-value, low-risk scenarios such as document processing and code generation, then expand toward customer interactions. In 2025 it spent US$18 billion on technology, of which about US$2 billion was AI-specific; CEO Jamie Dimon said the company "spent US$2 billion and got about US$2 billion in benefits" [official claims], positioning AI as a "bet-the-company" strategic investment.

**DBS: a pioneer in AI value accounting [official claims + third-party verified]**

More than 1,500 AI models deployed across 370-plus scenarios; in 2025 it created S$1 billion (about US$750 million) in AI value and was named "World's Best AI Bank" by Global Finance magazine. The key practice is transparently measuring AI value each year across three dimensions — "revenue uplift, cost savings, risk avoidance" — and using that to set investment priorities. This is the critical management infrastructure of the AI-native organization: linking AI investment directly to financial outcomes.

### 10.3.4 Failure Warnings

**Jane Street's US$15 billion loss [third-party verified]**

In July 2026, quantitative giant Jane Street suffered a monthly loss of nearly US$15 billion — its first monthly loss in about a decade — partly due to a deep drawdown at Situational Awareness, an AI hedge fund it invested in, compounded by missteps in directional Asia equity bets; around the same time, an AI-stock selloff triggered a chain reaction of margin calls at Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan Chase. There is a huge gap between the "AI stock-picking guru" narrative and real risk: AI capability amplifies tail risk, and model extrapolation and concentration risk do not disappear because of "intelligence."

**Klarna's pullback [third-party verified / CEO public interviews]**

Klarna cut its workforce from over 7,000 to under 3,000 (a reduction of more than 50%), with AI handling 2.3 million conversations a month — the workload of 700 full-time customer-service agents. But in May 2025 the CEO publicly admitted the company "went too far": an excessive focus on efficiency degraded service quality, forcing a pivot to an "Uber model" — AI handles standard issues while humans handle interactions requiring emotional connection — after which NPS and satisfaction recovered. Two lessons: AI customer service must read the original business code rather than FAQs; and "AI can answer ≠ AI can solve" — technical metrics and business metrics can diverge.

**The frontline temperature gap: statistics versus on-the-ground reality [third-party verified]**

The six big banks spend 130.091 billion yuan a year on fintech, and the whole industry has cumulatively invested nearly one trillion yuan — yet down at second-tier city branch level, the "felt experience is weak": AI applications remain mostly "point-embedded" and have not yet penetrated to restructure the underlying business process — "a better whip for the carriage rather than a new car." Some city commercial banks even prohibit using AI to generate discussion materials. Value metrics such as "person-years," "work-hours," and "scenario counts" are not comparable across institutions, frontline employees are becoming "AI trainers" (data labeling, effect feedback, scenario training), and "saving 15.56 million hours" is more about avoiding headcount increases than converting directly into headcount reduction.

### 10.3.5 Organizational Patterns and Consulting Takeaways

First, regulation determines the order of deployment. The sequence of AI adoption in finance is decided not by technical difficulty but by regulatory tolerance: intelligent customer service, document processing, and code generation go first; core decision links such as credit approval, asset pricing, and capital trading must wait until the governance framework matures. The EU AI Act requires high-risk financial AI to meet compliance requirements by August 2026 (with fines of up to 7% of global turnover for violations), and the NFRA guidance requires AI risk to be incorporated into the comprehensive risk-management system — compliance is not a barrier to transformation but its roadmap.

Second, data governance is a prerequisite, not an afterthought. In financial scenarios, model hallucination is a business incident, not a technical fault; discriminatory bias in historical data becomes institutionalized through models. Consulting takeaway: build model registration, risk grading, and human-supervision mechanisms first, then talk about scaling.

Third, talent-structure change is harder to manage than technological change. Foreign banks are densely creating Chief AI Officer roles (IBM research shows the share of organizations with a CAIO jumped from 26% to 76%), yet Standard Chartered's former CAIO believes the role is "rather short-lived" — "as AI becomes a tool like Excel." Organizational form is evolving toward a "pine-tree" model (in the view of Hengfeng Bank's CIO): a small number of core decision nodes support large-scale standardized execution, with middle management compressed. On talent strategy, the dual track of "replacement plus role creation" is more sustainable than pure layoffs: retained employees see pay increases (Klarna's per-capita pay rose nearly 50%), and transferred employees move into data and scenario-training roles.

Fourth, value accounting moves from "comparing inputs" to "comparing outcomes." After nearly one trillion yuan in cumulative industry investment, the banking AI race has entered its "calculate the ROI" second half; building a unified AI value-measurement system (DBS's three-dimension method is a reference) should be the first move in any consultation. AI-native transformation in finance has never been a technology-selection problem; it is an organizational problem of re-sequencing four things simultaneously: the regulatory framework, data governance, talent structure, and financial accounting.

## 10.4 Education

### 10.4.1 Industry Profile and Bottleneck Triage

Education is a typical "dual information-physical bottleneck" industry, with an information-to-physical ratio of about 70:30. Knowledge instruction, homework grading, Q&A and assessment, and admissions administration are all, at bottom, information processing — the most direct use case for large models. But the underlying mechanisms of learning outcomes — motivation, focus, peer encouragement, and teacher-student trust — still depend on in-person settings and human presence. This forms the first pair of core contradictions: teaching delivery can be productized, but the in-person setting is hard to replace. The second pair is the double-edged sword of equity: AI can amplify quality teaching resources at very low cost (GSU significantly improved first-generation college students' performance), but unequal access can widen the digital divide (LAUSD's high-profile project collapsed precisely here). The third pair is the tug-of-war between AI literacy and dependency: China lists teachers' digital literacy as a "basic item" of transformation, and the EU's AI Act turns AI literacy into a legal obligation; meanwhile, students substituting free general-purpose AI for paid services (the collapse of Chegg) forces the industry to redefine "what learning cannot be outsourced."

### 10.4.2 Transformation Paths and Typical Practices

Four paths are advancing in parallel.

**University organizational transformation**: In January 2024, ASU became the world's first university to partner comprehensively with OpenAI, embedding ChatGPT across teaching, research, and administration; OpenAI then released ChatGPT Edu for universities in May, turning universities into institutional clients. Georgia State University (GSU) embedded AI into the "student success" alert-remind-intervene chain. By late 2025, industry discourse shifted from "using AI tools" to "AI-native universities," with AI embedded across admissions, teaching, administration, and governance. But Ithaka S+R's interviews with 246 faculty at 17 US and Canadian universities show that many faculty do not even know about their school's existing AI resources — the bottleneck is not technology but organizational communication and trust.

**Evidence on AI tutors**: An Education Next forum synthesis gives balanced evidence: when expert teaching decisions were injected into GPT-4, 76% of its math responses were rated "good" by humans (the Bridge method), and AI-tutored students completed in 49 minutes a task that took the control group a 60-minute lecture; but researchers warn that publication bias is widespread, and most online math programs produce minimal gains for "95% of students."

**Education company transformation**: Duolingo sustains high growth through AI-driven course generation and personalization. Youdao (Chinese: 网易有道), built on its "Zi Yue" (Chinese: 子曰) large model, has repositioned its brand as a "learning and advertising AI application service provider"; AI subscription sales exceeded 100 million yuan in Q1 2026 (+70% year over year), and online marketing revenue surpassed the traditional education business for the first time. Squirrel Ai (Chinese: 松鼠Ai), which started with adaptive algorithms, had five papers accepted at ACL 2025 and over 100 papers at top conferences cumulatively; its adaptive system was named to TIME's 2025 Best Inventions. New Oriental launched "AI 1-on-1," Zuoyebang is betting on large-model learning devices, and Yuanfudao's (Chinese: 猿辅导) "Xiaoyuan AI" (Chinese: 小猿 AI) topped the new-download charts in its first month but has not closed the profitability loop. The 2025 split is clear: AI-native organizations earn a growth premium, while slow transformers slide into structural decline.

**China's policy path**: Unlike the West's institution-driven approach, China's is "state-driven, top-down, scaled rollout." In March 2025, the Ministry of Education deployed Strategic Action 2.0 themed "AI and Education Transformation"; in April, nine government departments issued the Opinions on Accelerating the Digitalization of Education; in April 2026, five departments jointly issued the "AI + Education" Action Plan (Jiaokexin [2026] No. 1), proposing to form a deeply integrated AI-education landscape and universalize AI education in primary and secondary schools by 2030 — elevating AI from an "information tool" to a "systemic restructuring force." Supporting measures: in July 2025, the Ministry of Education issued the Digital Empowerment of Teacher Development Action, proposing to make teachers' digital literacy the "new normal" within three to five years, exploring large-scale individualized instruction and human-AI collaborative teaching, and opening AI-literacy training programs for teachers in central and western China; the China Society of Education is building a best-practice diffusion network through an "AI + Basic Education" case collection. The EU took a third path: the AI Act classifies scoring, admission, and assessment education AI as high-risk, mandating human oversight and AI-literacy training — regulation reshaping the organization in reverse.

### 10.4.3 Benchmark Cases

**Georgia State University's Pounce: embedding AI into the "student success" process [third-party verified]**. Pounce is the most evidence-backed AI application in education: in its first summer it interacted with incoming freshmen 185,000 times; a randomized controlled trial showed that students who received conversational reminders raised their FAFSA completion and fall enrollment rates by 3 percentage points and cut fee-related withdrawal by 50%; the classroom version of the chatbot helped students in an introductory political science course earn B-or-better grades at a rate 16 percentage points higher than the control group, with first-generation students averaging 11 points higher on final exams. The key is not the model but the organizational design: AI does not replace teachers; it scales advisory services at very low cost.

**Duolingo: the growth premium and organizational cost of being AI-native [third-party verified]**. Its personalization engine Birdbrain has been running for over a decade; on April 30, 2025, it used AI to generate 148 courses in a single day, doubling its course catalog. The financial payoff of the AI-first strategy is significant: full-year 2025 revenue of US$1.038 billion (+39%), 52.7 million DAU (+30%), and a 29.5% adjusted EBITDA margin. But the leak of an internal "AI-first" memo in May 2025 triggered an uproar, Q2 growth fell from 60% to 40%, and the stock dropped from a high of US$544.93 to about US$95 by March 2026 — becoming AI-native brings a growth premium, but it also amplifies the communication and capital risks of organizational change.

**Khan Academy: experiment-culture-driven AI tutor iteration [official claims]**. From October 2025 to April 2026, the Khanmigo team ran systematic product experiments across more than 15 million tutoring sessions: feeding the model the student's structured recent problem-solving history improved "next-question accuracy" by 6.1%; simplifying model output cut average response time by 3 seconds while math accuracy held steady; adding skill-session records from the past 24 hours raised cognitive engagement by 5.09%. The organization candidly admits "no single improvement brings a leap," but combined optimization significantly improves outcomes and lowers cost — this is the core engineering capability of an AI-native education organization: an experiment culture plus large-scale A/B testing.

### 10.4.4 Failure Warnings

**The collapse of LAUSD's Ed: narrative first, governance absent [third-party verified]**. The AI chatbot "Ed," built by the Los Angeles Unified School District at a cost of millions of dollars and billed as a "learning acceleration platform," was finally taken offline after vendor AllHere imploded and its CEO departed, and it drew in an FBI investigation related to contract procurement; the district superintendent resigned in June 2026. Technology selection and PR narrative came first, while vendor due diligence, data security, procurement compliance, and exit mechanisms were absent — public money and public trust were damaged at the same time.

**Chegg: displaced by free general-purpose AI [third-party verified]**. In May 2025, Chegg announced layoffs of about 22% (248 people) and closed its US and Canada offices; Q1 subscribers fell 31% year over year to 3.2 million, and revenue fell 30%. This unicorn, once valued at US$12 billion, saw its "homework answers plus textbook rental" business model directly displaced by free tools such as ChatGPT — the paradigm shock suffered by a non-AI-native organization.

**2U: the bankruptcy of an asset-heavy model [third-party verified]**. In July 2024, online education operator 2U — once valued at over US$5 billion — filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy, cutting debt from US$945 million to US$459 million; court documents attributed the decline in part to "the rapid and unexpected adoption of artificial intelligence," and new OPM partnerships fell 53% in 2023–2024. A high-leverage old model dependent on degree outsourcing lacked organizational resilience.

**Khanmigo's low adoption: supply is not adoption [third-party verified]**. In April 2026, Sal Khan admitted that Khanmigo is "a non-event" for most students, with deep engagement at only about 15%; the Digital Education Council's 2026 global survey found that only 5% of students who experienced classroom AI said it "changed the way they learn"; a randomized controlled study in a university physics course also failed to reproduce significant advantages. The "access → use → learning outcome" three-stage funnel leaks at every stage — being technology-native does not mean being organization-native.

### 10.4.5 Organizational Patterns and Consulting Takeaways

First, productizable teaching delivery and in-person scenarios must be managed in separate accounts. Productizable steps — instruction, grading, Q&A, administration — should be AI-first and measured for returns; in-person steps — motivation, companionship, social connection, and teacher-student trust — stay with humans. GSU's success came from embedding AI into the process; Khanmigo's lesson is treating AI as a technology that "works the moment it's injected."

Second, teachers are the gateway to organizational change. The resistance is not a technology problem but a meaning problem: having teachers do "value-added/non-value-added task analysis" and showing them AI's value to their professional identity works better than tool training; the resource-information gap Ithaka revealed shows that communication and incentives must precede technology deployment. China institutionalizes teacher-literacy training, and the EU mandates AI literacy by law — different roads, same destination: teacher capability building is the transformation's infrastructure.

Third, "supply ≠ adoption"; adoption engineering matters as much as the product. Free and embedded-in-platform does not earn usage either — it requires the three-piece set of teacher guidance, classroom process, and incentive structure to be designed together; Khan Academy's experiment culture proves that outcomes come from continuous iteration, not a one-time deployment.

Fourth, identify the two institutional paths and design consulting plans separately. China is policy-driven, with the levers being action plans, platform scaling, and case-library diffusion, focused on compliant rollout and regional scaling; the West is institution-driven piloting, with the levers being teacher adoption, outcome evidence, and governance frameworks (such as the EU AI Act), focused on single-institution change design and RCT-style evidence production.

Fifth, treat outcome evidence in tiers. Official claims (Khan's experiments, Squirrel Ai's TIME listing, IDC rating it L5 fully adaptive) and third-party RCTs (GSU, the Khanmigo physics course) must be checked side by side, and beware of publication bias and vendor marketing materials; when attributing outcomes, place "pedagogical design" before "model capability" — with the same model, the way it is embedded determines the result.

## 10.5 Retail & Consumer Goods

### 10.5.1 Industry Profile and Bottleneck Triage

Retail is an industry with "dual information and physical bottlenecks," at a ratio of about 60:40. Half is an information business (demand forecasting, assortment, marketing, membership operations) and half is a physical business (stores, shelves, warehouses, cold chains, more than a million employees). Bain & Company data show that leading retailers using AI for personalized marketing saw ROAS improvements of 10%–25% in early experiments [third-party verified]; but the physical-side limits are just as real — when Amazon shut down Amazon Go, it admitted that logistics and technological excellence alone are "far from enough; you also need merchandising and display skills."

The core contradictions are threefold. First, online information flow versus offline store physics: what consumers see online and what the store actually carries are two systems that have long operated in silos — offline fulfillment remains the decisive battleground of cost and experience. Second, supply-chain efficiency versus experience: SHEIN compressed the industry-average unsold inventory rate of about 30% into single digits, while McDonald's AI ordering failed precisely because "efficiency wasn't saved and the experience collapsed first." Third, algorithmic site selection versus organizational inertia: more than 70% of Luckin's new stores are sited by algorithmic recommendation, yet most chains' expansion is still driven by their development teams' "experience plus relationships" — the handover of power between algorithms and the organization is the hardest bone in retail AI transformation.

The China Chain Store & Franchise Association's (CCFA) consensus is that "organizational change is the biggest challenge for AI deployment" [third-party verified]; industry case surveys likewise show the pattern of "marketing before supply chain, single points before the full chain" [third-party verified].

### 10.5.2 Transformation Paths and Typical Practices

Retail AI transformation can be split into at least four paths.

**International giants: "human-machine collaboration" and "dual-track ecosystems."** Walmart deployed AI tools (real-time translation, task management) for about 1.5 million US store associates and used generative AI to rewrite 850 million product catalog entries — making AI an "exoskeleton of productivity" for frontline employees rather than a reason for layoffs [official claims]. Target integrated its app with ChatGPT for personalized shopping while rolling out ChatGPT Enterprise to all employees [official claims]. Carrefour was the earliest to embed generative AI across the full chain: its Hopla shopping assistant covers all online channels, more than 2,000 products use AI-generated copy, and it was the first European retailer to open shopping inside ChatGPT [official claims]. Starbucks represents the "AI-augmented organization": Deep Brew sits in the back office of scheduling, stocking, and predictive maintenance, freeing people for human-facing service [third-party verified]. Amazon pushes AI down to the physical layer of fulfillment: more than 1 million robots are orchestrated by generative AI, and new-generation facilities are expected to automate 50–60% of tasks [third-party verified].

**Supply-chain AI: "trading data for certainty."** SHEIN's "small-batch, fast-response" model compressed the industry-average unsold inventory rate of about 30% into single digits; 2025 revenue was US$41.8 billion, and it ran nearly 600 supplier training sessions with 37,000 person-times [third-party verified]. Inditex plans to invest €1.8 billion in technology and logistics; AI demand forecasting already drives 85% of initial production quota decisions, and FY2025 sales reached €39.9 billion, up 7% at constant exchange rates [third-party verified]. Uniqlo's "Ariake Project" (Chinese: 有明计划) invests about ¥100 billion in RFID item-level tracking, AI forecasting connected directly to factories, and warehouse automation [third-party verified].

**Chinese chain organizational change: fix the organization first, then rebuild efficiency.** In April 2026, Hema (Chinese: 盒马) consolidated 19 regions into 9 large regions, set up an AI committee, and created AI BP roles [third-party verified]; Sam's Club (Chinese: 山姆), by contrast, split its regions in the opposite direction — organizational structure must match the business stage, and the direction can legitimately differ [third-party verified]. RT-Mart (Chinese: 大润发), after DCP Capital took control, has "returned to the essence of retail": FY2025 returned to profit (net profit of 386 million yuan after a 1.668 billion yuan loss the prior year), with a plan to renovate 500 stores over three years [third-party verified].

**Consumer brands: AI in service of the business model.** Mixue's (Chinese: 蜜雪冰城) "Snow King AI Brain" (Chinese: 雪王 AI 大脑) turns AI into the cost-control system of its value-pricing strategy — with a gross margin of only about 35%, technology is not showing off but a survival necessity [third-party verified]; Pop Mart (Chinese: 泡泡玛特) built its self-developed AI integration platform HeyLisa, using AI sales forecasting to convert "hit-product uncertainty" into a predictable supply chain [third-party verified]; 7-Eleven Japan is the model of "digitize first, then make intelligent": data assets accumulated over decades of item-level management make AI demand forecasting viable [third-party verified].

### 10.5.3 Benchmark Cases

**IKEA Billie: the unsolved problems AI reveals are worth more than the problems it solves [third-party verified]**

Ingka Group launched customer-service chatbot Billie in 2021; from 2021 to 2023 it handled 3.2 million interactions, resolved about 47% of inquiries, and saved about €13 million in customer-service costs. The pivotal turn: IKEA did not lay off the roughly 8,500 customer-service agents displaced by AI. Instead, it analyzed the 53% of inquiries Billie could not resolve and discovered that what customers really wanted was "does this sofa fit my home" — an interior-design consulting need that had long been ignored. IKEA retrained the 8,500 agents as remote interior-design consultants, who generated €1.3 billion in revenue in their first full year (about 3.3% of group revenue). This is the most important positive sample of AI-native organizational transformation: the unsolved problems AI reveals are worth more than the problems it solves, and the freed-up workforce is redeployed to higher-value work — organizational transformation is a technology decision and a talent decision happening at the same time.

**Luckin: algorithmic site selection and the "Smart Brew ecosystem" [official presentation; data not independently verified]**

As of May 31, 2026, Luckin had 35,000+ stores worldwide, 473 million cumulative transacting customers, and 4.1 billion freshly made beverages sold in 2025. Three hard metrics: more than 70% of new store locations are algorithm-recommended, with an average of 18 days from application to acceptance; 700,000+ IoT-connected devices with a 99% online rate; and smart ordering is twice as efficient. Its self-developed agent, AI Lucky, has been integrated into super-apps including Qwen, WeChat, and Alipay and supports voice ordering; Luckin is also building the "Zhicui Ecosystem" (Chinese: 智萃生态圈) with multiple cloud vendors, upgrading AI from a point tool to a platform capability.

**Hema: the governance package of an AI committee and a regional system [third-party verified]**

In April 2026, Hema completed its organizational upgrade from a city-based system to a regional system, consolidating 19 regions into 9 large regions; in parallel, it set up an AI committee led by the CTO and created AI BP roles, directly connecting AI capability with business needs. The backdrop: nine consecutive months of overall profitability in 2024 and its first full-year positive adjusted EBITA in FY2025 (as reviewed by EqualOcean, Chinese: 亿欧). Hema advanced organizational change and AI governance structure in tandem — "committee plus BP roles" is the common mechanism linking the technology center with business units.

### 10.5.4 Failure Warnings

**Amazon Go/Fresh closures: technological leadership does not equal a viable business model [third-party verified]**

In January 2026, Amazon announced it would close all 58 Fresh supermarkets and 14 Go stores, accompanied by 16,000 layoffs, admitting it had "not created a sufficiently distinctive customer experience or the right economic model." Go's Just Walk Out "grab-and-go" technology was dazzling, but it could not mask mediocre merchandising; tellingly, Just Walk Out succeeded as a technology sold to 360+ third-party locations. The lesson: retail AI must solve "differentiated experience plus unit economics" at the same time — in the wrong business model, technology is only an expensive decoration.

**McDonald's × IBM voice ordering: the accuracy-cost-experience triple threshold [third-party verified]**

In June 2024, McDonald's ended the AI voice-ordering test it had run with IBM across 100+ stores since 2021. Analysts noted accuracy of only 80–85%, below the 95% threshold, and operating costs higher than human staff — and a viral TikTok video of "AI getting a McNuggets order wrong" amplified the experience collapse. The lesson: retail AI deployments must clear the "accuracy-cost-experience" triple threshold; there is a huge gap between technology demos and real operating scenarios.

**Yonghui learns from Pangdonglai: easy to copy the form, hard to copy the spirit [third-party verified]**

After more than two years of store renovations, Yonghui's renovated stores average over 80% foot-traffic growth per store — yet the company still posted a 2025 net loss attributable to shareholders of 2.550 billion yuan, with cumulative losses approaching 12.05 billion yuan from 2021 to 2025. The comparison data are stark: Pangdonglai (Chinese: 胖东来) employees average monthly salaries above 9,500 yuan with a turnover rate of only 0.33%; Yonghui's renovated-store employees earn 4,500–6,000 yuan a month — 65% of Pangdonglai's pay for the same position — and there is no profit-sharing mechanism. Pangdonglai's essence is a unique ordering of scale, profit, and employee relations; a nationwide chain cannot replicate its regional supply-chain density. "Whole-company financials versus isolated store performance" being worlds apart is one of the deepest lessons in organizational transformation.

### 10.5.5 Organizational Patterns and Consulting Takeaways

First, retail's AI-native transformation is, at bottom, a re-orchestration of two systems: the "information flow" and the "physical flow." Online-offline integration is not about moving products onto the web; it is about letting demand signals (online searches, membership data) and supply actions (store replenishment, production scheduling) share the same data foundation. The first move of any consultation should be to inventory whether item-level data assets exist and whether they are connected — 7-Eleven proves that AI's moat is not the model but the data discipline of "item-level management."

Second, supply-chain data foundations determine the AI ceiling. SHEIN's small-batch fast response, Zara's 85% of production quotas decided by AI, and Uniqlo's end-to-end RFID all point to the same thing: supply-chain AI's effectiveness depends on the depth of the industry chain's digitalization, not on model parameter count. Retail AI is an "asset-heavy" transformation — trading data infrastructure for certainty in inventory and cash flow.

Third, store employee roles must be rebuilt, not eliminated. IKEA retrained 8,500 agents, Walmart gave AI tools to 1.5 million associates, Amazon expects about 25% of its workforce to shift into technology-enabled roles, and 7-Eleven uses AI avatars to cope with labor shortages — all point to a "human-machine hybrid" organizational form. Layoff-style AI transformation in retail directly destroys the physical-side asset of the in-store experience.

Fourth, consumer trust is the invisible ceiling of retail AI. Walmart's research shows that consumers' trust in AI recommendations (27%) has overtaken influencer commerce (24%) — but missing privacy protections can void the transformation promise [official claims].

Fifth, organizational structure must match the business stage and digital maturity. Hema merging regions (seeking scale in an expansion phase) and Sam's Club splitting regions (seeking flexibility in a high-growth phase) moved in opposite directions and both worked; Yonghui's "easy to copy the form, hard to copy the spirit" shows that copying a benchmark's surface practices without adjusting the profit-distribution mechanism leaves the transformation stuck in isolated lines of the financial statements. The point of retail AI consulting is never model selection; it is the simultaneous re-sequencing of the data foundation, organizational structure, and incentive mechanism.
## 10.6 Government & Public Services

### 10.6.1 Industry Profile and Bottleneck Triage

Government and public services is one of the few industries where the "information and physical bottlenecks" are split roughly fifty-fifty: approval processing, policy consultation, document drafting, and service-request routing are typically information-intensive links, while counter services, on-site law enforcement, grid patrols, and urban infrastructure operations remain firmly bound to the physical world — in sharp contrast to finance's 80:20 information-dominant structure. This determines that government AI transformation must be a dual-track overhaul of "online intelligence + offline coordination," rather than a purely digital reconstruction.

Its core contradiction has three layers. First, the paradox of data security and sharing: government data is scattered across departments, with unclear powers and responsibilities and poor circulation — "data silos" have long been the No. 1 pain point; the national government's creation of a data administration is precisely aimed at breaking this deadlock, yet local practice generally finds "consolidation easy, coordination hard" — data-management responsibilities have not been synchronized with powers over project approval, funding, and staffing. Second, digital formalism: the CAC's deployment guidelines singled out for criticism "blindly pursuing technological superiority, duplicate construction, and forced usage," and People's Daily Online also warns against bandwagon construction "for the sake of using AI." Third, the decision-accountability chain: once AI-generated official documents, replies, or handling recommendations go wrong, there are no mature institutions for responsibility assignment, human fallback, or algorithmic transparency. The mirror-image dilemma exists in the US federal government as well — the Brookings Institution found AI use cases there highly concentrated in a few large agencies, with uneven diffusion, and procurement and budget mechanisms constituting structural barriers.

### 10.6.2 Transformation Paths and Typical Practices

Chinese government AI transformation shows a clear "policy-chain-driven" character: "AI+" was written into the Government Work Report in 2024; in August 2025 the State Council issued the *Opinions on Deepening the Implementation of the "AI+" Action*; and in October of the same year the CAC released the *Guidelines for the Deployment and Application of AI Large Models in the Government Sector* — forming a complete chain of "top-level design — special action — scenario guidelines."

The core of the deployment route is "centralized, consolidated planning": the guidelines specify that counties and below should in principle reuse higher-level compute and model resources rather than independently build government LLMs, exploring "build in one place, reuse across many regions and departments" to prevent "model silos." At the same time, they delineate four major scenarios — government services, social governance, agency office work, and decision support — and emphasize the LLM's "assistive" positioning and the discipline that "classified information never goes online." DeepSeek's open-sourcing, with its low cost, high performance, and easy deployment, dramatically lowered the threshold for adoption, and domestic adaptation (Ascend chips, all-in-one appliances) became the standard.

At the organizational level, the National Data Administration (Chinese: 国家数据局, established in October 2023) has become the "organizational hub" of digital transformation: 2024 was positioned as the "year of institution building," shifting to the "year of reform breakthroughs" in 2025, driving the development and use of public data and the "Data Elements ×" initiative, with 23 provincial-level data administrations formed by the end of 2024. In terms of implementation forms, the "City Brain" has been elevated from a technology platform to a city-level governance mechanism reconstruction — Chongqing, relying on its integrated intelligent public data platform, automatically generated response plans such as police deployment and subway flow control through 5G + AI video analytics when daily footfall in the Jiefangbei business district exceeded 1.8 million, achieving "second-level sensing — intelligent assessment — coordinated response." On the grassroots governance side, Chongqing's "141" system embeds AI across 1,031 townships and streets and 62,000 grids, while Xiaoshan, Shangcheng, Nanshan, and Yuhang have formed closed loops of "AI collection — human handling — data feedback." The 12345 hotline has become the densest entry point for agents, with Guangzhou, Suzhou, and Jinan all deploying full chains of "smart form-filling — knowledge recommendation — smart dispatch." Shanghai's "Smart Easy Handling 4.0" (Chinese: 智慧好办 4.0) and Guangdong's "provincial coordination + municipal pilots + open scenario challenges" represent provincial-level promotion mechanisms.

### 10.6.3 Benchmark Cases

**Shenzhen Futian: 70 AI "Digital Workers" [official claims + third-party verified]**

Launched in February 2025, the first batch of 70 digital workers is built on DeepSeek fused with local knowledge bases, covering 11 major scenario categories and 240 government scenario terminals, forming a closed loop of "demand — training — scenario application — iteration." Document-formatting correction accuracy exceeds 95%, review time is cut by 90%, and the error rate is kept within 5%; law-enforcement transcripts are converted into draft documents in seconds; routing accuracy for public livelihood requests improved from 70% to 95%; personalized custom generation was compressed from 5 days to minutes. A unified management platform handles full-lifecycle management of these digital workers, making this a landmark implementation of the "AI civil servant" concept.

**Shenzhen's "Shen Xiao-i" (Chinese: 深小i): A Public-Facing Government Agent [official claims]**

Launched on February 22, 2025, it is the country's first practical AI government assistant open to the public, embedded in the full "ask, search, guide, process, review" service flow. It has cumulatively handled over 4 million intelligent Q&A sessions, with a response rate above 97% and first-answer accuracy above 94%. It is one of the first 7 government agents to pass evaluation by CAICT's TEL Laboratory (Chinese: 信通院泰尔实验室) and was selected as a standard application case of "AI + government" by the CAC. Behind it is Shenzhen's AI platform: managing 270P of compute, 39 deployed models, and 97.38 million annual calls, with 10 scenario challenges organized through open-competition recruitment (Chinese: 揭榜挂帅) — platform-based supply plus scenario-based open challenges brings it closer to "AI-native" than single-point applications.

**Guangzhou: China's First Domestically Adapted Government DeepSeek [official / third-party verified]**

Adaptation was completed and rolled out broadly in January 2025: the 12345 hotline answers 50,000 calls daily, and the intelligent agent-seat system built in early 2023 has cut caller waiting time by 43% and achieved 97% accuracy in work-order routing; the Digital Guangzhou Innovation Laboratory has adapted over 150 LLMs and opened the country's first government-grade compute center running DeepSeek-R1 671B on Ascend; the Guangzhou Intermediate People's Court deployed the country's first private government DeepSeek all-in-one appliance; Yuexiu District's "Ai Xiaoyue" (Chinese: 艾小越) achieves 99% answer accuracy. Guangzhou has proposed "one ledger, one mechanism, one platform" for LLMs, building a two-way loop in which "data feeds AI, and AI feeds decisions back."

### 10.6.4 Failure Warnings

**AI Hallucination: Errors in Government Are Irreversible [third-party verified]**

*Outlook Weekly* (Chinese: 瞭望) dissects three causes of hallucination: insufficient or biased training data, algorithmic architecture limitations, and the probabilistic nature of generation. LLMs "confidently spouting nonsense" can cause erroneous decisions and damage to public trust in highly regulated domains such as government and the judiciary — once a mistaken policy interpretation reaches the public, it constitutes real harm. CAICT experts point out that hallucination governance requires both "technology and institutions": retrieval augmentation and fact-checking on the technical side, and content review, risk warnings, and accountability tracing on the institutional side. This is precisely why the CAC guidelines require prominent risk warnings in the interface.

**The GAO Audit: Governance Lagging Behind Deployment [third-party verified]**

In its July 2025 review of 12 federal agencies, the US Government Accountability Office (GAO) found incomplete governance frameworks, use-case inventories not updated in time, and inadequate risk management for generative-AI-specific issues such as hallucination, data leakage, and copyright. US federal AI use cases grew fivefold in five years to 3,611, yet governance cannot keep pace with the speed of diffusion. The GAO's framework of "use-case inventory + risk classification + accountability auditing" suggests: build governance first, then talk about scale.

**Digital Formalism: Advanced Technology, Lagging Experience [third-party verified]**

The CAC guidelines explicitly require guarding against digital formalism; People's Daily Online criticizes bandwagon construction "for the sake of using AI"; the magazine *Qunzhong (Mass)* (Chinese: 群众) warns of "advanced technology, lagging experience." The more hidden failure is organizational: mismatched powers and responsibilities of local data administrations leave coordination spinning in place, while US federal AI use cases are highly concentrated and imperceptible at the grassroots. Government AI usually fails not because the models are inadequate, but because governance and organization have not kept up.

### 10.6.5 Organizational Patterns and Consulting Takeaways

First, **institutions-first determines the order of deployment.** The rollout sequence of government AI is set by policy and security boundaries rather than technical difficulty: the CAC guidelines are both a constraint and a roadmap, and its three principles — scenario classification, centralized consolidated planning, and the assistive role — should be the preconditions for initiating any government AI project.

Second, **the assistive role is both politically correct and engineering-correct.** The stable form of government AI is the "digital colleague" rather than the "digital replacement": repetitive, standardized work goes to AI, while value judgments, emotional communication, and discretionary authority stay with people, with "human fallback" as the final line of defense against hallucination risk.

Third, **the data administration is the real lever of organizational change.** Government AI transformation is essentially a "data-administration-driven organizational rearrangement": the restructuring of tiao-kuai (vertical-line and horizontal-block) relations across national, provincial, and city/county levels, paired with organizational instruments such as leading groups, standardization technical committees, big-data professional-title review, and zero-based budgeting (as in Shenzhen), determines success or failure more than any single technology procurement.

Fourth, **grassroots burden reduction must be measured.** Shenzhen Nanshan's AI identified 87,000 public requests, improved key-information accuracy by 30%, and captured 820 weak risk signals — burden reduction backed by data is the only real "burden reduction," while forced usage and duplicate form-filling are a new form of formalism. The first consulting move should be helping clients build the governance foundation of "use-case inventory + risk classification + accountability auditing" (modeled on the GAO framework and CAICT's case-evaluation mechanism), and unifying value metrics such as "number of scenarios, hours saved, accuracy" — otherwise government AI will repeat the failure of "statistical prosperity, frontline indifference."

## 10.7 Logistics & Supply Chain

### 10.7.1 Industry Profile and Bottleneck Triage

In logistics and supply chain, the information-to-physical bottleneck ratio is roughly 40:60 — the exact opposite of finance (80:20), and this is the key to understanding the industry's AI transformation pace. Beneath the asset-heavy surface, what drives the network is decision information: forecasting, scheduling, routing, and risk control. CFLP data shows China's logistics industry total revenue reached RMB 13.2 trillion in 2023; industry estimates put the global AI smart-logistics market above US$42.8 billion in 2025, with over 1.5 million warehouse robots deployed [third-party verified].

But the industry's AI transformation faces three structural contradictions. First, automation is a heavy-asset investment while demand is highly volatile: an automated terminal costs billions, takes years to build, yet demand fluctuates week by week — capital expenditure and capacity utilization are structurally mismatched. Second, right-of-way and compliance constraints in the physical world: autonomous delivery vehicles "have reached the ten-thousand-unit scale, yet are still waiting for a national pass" [third-party verified], and fragmented right-of-way policies directly draw the commercial boundary of unmanned capacity. Third, technical maturity does not equal commercial maturity: L4 autonomous trucks have been explored for over a decade without a working unit-economics model, and drone order volumes coexist with mounting losses. A CFLP report and a *People's Tribune* (Chinese: 人民论坛) article respectively attribute the barriers to insufficient data quality and standardization, missing cross-department coordination, AI talent shortages, and immature input-output measurement systems — as well as high upgrade costs, data silos, privacy compliance, and policy support — and warn of "algorithmic black boxes" and the "intelligence monopoly" risk of excluding small and medium enterprises. Logistics does not lack AI pilots; it lacks the organizational capability and commercial closed loop to turn pilots into systems.

### 10.7.2 Transformation Paths and Typical Practices

Logistics AI deployment unfolds along five paths, with value realization on the information layer generally outpacing the physical layer.

**Express logistics LLMs.** Leading companies have formed a combined play of "vertical LLM + agents + full-chain scenarios" [third-party verified]: Cainiao (Chinese: 菜鸟) launched a logistics customer-service AI brain in 2018; STO Express (Chinese: 申通) launched "Kunlun" in 2021; 2023 saw a burst of releases — Cainiao's "Tianji π," JD.com's "Superbrain" (Chinese: 超脑), and YTO Express's "Zhiduoxing" (Chinese: 智多星); SF Express (Chinese: 顺丰) released its "Fengzhi" (Chinese: 丰知) decision LLM in 2024; and JD upgraded to "Superbrain 2.0" and released the "Yilang" (Chinese: 异狼) robotic arm in 2025 [official claims]. The strategic split is clear: the direct-operated camp (SF Express, JD.com) develops full-stack in-house, while the franchise camp uses AI tools to penetrate the "headquarters—outlet—courier" coordination chain — YTO has embedded over 100 agents across its full chain, generating cost savings on the order of RMB 1 billion, and AI roles have gone through a three-stage upgrade from "agent" to "AI assistant" to "digital worker" [official claims + third-party verified].

**Warehouse robots.** Amazon announced in 2025 that its global robot deployments exceeded 1 million units, two-thirds of the global total [official claims]; Walmart is deeply tied to Symbotic, rolling AI automation across all 42 regional distribution centers within eight years; UPS aims to raise the share of US packages processed with automation from 66.5% to 68% by the end of 2026 [third-party verified]. Even the giants advance in "percentage-point" increments — transformation is a gradual engineering process, not a one-step "dark warehouse."

**Smart ports.** Qingdao Port (Chinese: 青岛港) built its "smart brain" on fully domestic, fully autonomous A-TOS/A-ECS systems, with 85 AGVs and 47 yard rail-mounted gantry cranes running unmanned end to end; Yangshan Phase IV operates on "F5G ultra-remote control + AGV automated handling," and the Port of Shanghai handled its 50 millionth TEU in November 2025, ranking first globally for 16 consecutive years [third-party verified]. COSCO Shipping (Chinese: 中远海运) forms an SOE three-tier approach with its Hi-Dolphin shipping LLM and L4 port autonomous driving [official claims].

**Autonomous delivery.** The industry delivered over 35,000 unmanned vehicles in 2025, and JD Logistics (Chinese: 京东物流) announced procurement of 1 million units over the next five years [third-party verified]; Meituan's commercial drone orders exceeded 670,000 and surpassed 900,000 after routine operation [official claims]; SF's Fengyi (Chinese: 丰翼) "Ark" (Chinese: 方舟) drone delivers over 20,000 packages per day.

**Digital freight platforms.** Full Truck Alliance (Chinese: 满帮) fulfilled over 236 million orders on its platform in 2025, compressing truck-to-cargo matching from hours to minutes, and its smart consolidated-order assignment saved shippers over RMB 18 billion in freight costs in a single year; Huolala (Chinese: 货拉拉; Lalamove internationally) drivers achieve a 95% on-time rate and a 92% freight matching rate [third-party verified].

### 10.7.3 Benchmark Cases

**Qingdao Port: An AI-Native Sample of Physical Heavy Assets [official claims + third-party verified].** On May 22, 2025, the quay cranes at Qingdao Port's Qianwan automated terminal reached an average efficiency of 62.62 containers per crane-hour, setting the world record for automated terminal handling efficiency for the 13th time. The core is the in-house A-TOS/A-ECS systems working together as a "smart brain," upgraded with AI LLMs for algorithms and compute. The terminal was approved in 2013 and construction began in 2015 — a decade of continuous iteration from an "unmanned terminal" to "full-domain intelligence." Port AI-nativeness is a systems engineering effort of "in-house foundation + continuous iteration + organizational support," not a single-point equipment purchase.

**Amazon: A Robot Army Plus a Central AI Brain [official claims, limited independent verification].** Starting with the Kiva acquisition in 2012, Amazon expanded over 13 years to 300+ warehouses and 1 million robots, becoming in 2025 the "world's largest mobile robot manufacturer and operator." The supporting DeepFleet generative-AI scheduling system coordinates robot movement in real time like an "intelligent traffic management system," reportedly improving fleet efficiency by 10% and overall logistics efficiency by 25% [official claims]. What deserves attention is not the numbers but the organizational logic: in-house development plus M&A builds a two-tier architecture of "robot army + central AI brain," and the real strategy is human-robot collaboration and job-skill reshaping.

**Full Truck Alliance: An Agent Upgrade for a Digital-Native Species [third-party verified + company disclosures].** A road-freight platform is a natively digital species for which "data is the business," and its evolutionary ladder — information matching (1.0), algorithmic matching (2.0), decision-proxy agents (3.0) — offers a template for comparison. Full Truck Alliance fulfilled over 236 million orders on its platform in 2025, and in 2026 is embedding agents into real transaction-decision scenarios with products like the "cargo-finding assistant" and "intelligent assistant," responding to fairness governance with algorithm disclosure [third-party verified].

### 10.7.4 Failure Warnings

**TuSimple: The Collapse of a Technology Leader's Commercial Closed Loop [third-party verified].** Dubbed "the world's first autonomous-driving stock," its market cap once reached US$16 billion; it was delisted in 2024, and its Guangzhou team was dissolved and liquidated in February 2025. Founder Chen Mo admitted that "from 2021 to now, the resources at TuSimple's disposal have been insufficient to complete the commercialization of autonomous driving." Three root causes: insufficient technical maturity, regulations not ready, and unit economics that did not work — the labor costs saved could not offset sensor and safety-operator costs; on top of that came governance failures of management infighting and strategic flip-flopping. An AI-native organization needs not only technical capability but also a sustainable commercial closed loop and stable organizational governance.

**The 95% Pilot Failure Rate [third-party verified].** project44, citing MIT research, notes that 95% of enterprise AI pilots fail to produce measurable returns (global AI spending exceeded US$420 billion in 2025). Its conclusion: "it's not a model problem, it's an execution problem" — poor workflow integration, agents lacking operational context, fragmented tools, prompt-engineering burden, and unclear accountability. In supply chains the cost is more direct: there is only a 2-hour window to handle a shipment exception, and once it closes, the company faces a forced choice among expedited premiums, customer damage, and profit pressure. Pilots fail not because of algorithms but because of process integration and accountability mechanisms.

**Autonomous Delivery: Scale Coexisting with Losses [official claims + third-party verified].** Meituan's commercial drone orders grew from 670,000 to 900,000, yet Meituan's new initiatives segment posted an operating loss of RMB 10.1 billion in 2025 (including drones); the unmanned-vehicle industry delivered over 35,000 units in 2025 with an estimated market headroom of nearly RMB 500 billion, yet remains stuck in "scaling at a loss" price wars and fragmented right-of-way policies. Technical validation is complete; commercial validation is unfinished — "technical maturity ≠ commercial maturity" is the most expensive lesson in logistics AI.

### 10.7.5 Organizational Patterns and Consulting Takeaways

First, **the physical bottleneck determines the order of value realization.** Logistics' 40:60 information-to-physical structure means "information-layer" AI — forecasting, scheduling, matching — realizes value fast, while "physical-layer" automation is an asset-heavy, long-cycle engineering effort. The first consulting move is to identify which layer the money is going into: the information layer runs on algorithms and data, the physical layer runs on capital and supply chain — with completely different rhythms.

Second, **data-driven scheduling organizations are the core form.** Agents are beginning to take on cross-department workflows, and organizations are shifting from "human + system" to "human + agent collaboration" [third-party verified], with AI roles evolving along "tool → assistant → digital worker." The key proposition for franchise networks is distributed coordination across "headquarters—outlet—courier": AI products must lower their entry barriers and adapt to the uneven digital capabilities of franchisees — organizational coordination efficiency, rather than mere labor replacement, is the value anchor of franchise-camp AI.

Third, **the three routes of in-house development, M&A, and vendor lock-in are clearly differentiated:** Amazon develops in-house and acquires; Walmart "divested in-house development and outsourced operations" (selling its robotics business for long-term orders worth over US$50 billion); Cainiao productized its logistics technology for external clients (800+ projects worldwide, 26 Fortune Global 500 clients). Whether an organization must develop AI in-house depends on its data assets and capital strength — "owning AI" and "running an AI-powered operation" are two equally legitimate choices.

Fourth, **commercial validation should come first.** McKinsey's *The State of AI 2025* shows 88% of organizations now use AI routinely, but only 39% capture significant value and 6% are high-performing winners [third-party verified]; the common root of TuSimple's collapse and the unmanned-delivery losses is large-scale investment before unit economics were validated. Pilots should be tied to business KPIs, not technical metrics.

Fifth, **talent and the industry foundation come first.** The center of gravity of supply chain competition is shifting from "buying systems" to "building capabilities" [third-party verified], generating new roles such as AI operations, data annotation, and human-machine collaboration supervision. At the same time, logistics AI-nativeness is a cross-entity systems engineering effort — the first national standard for logistics enterprise digitalization was released in September 2025, and the *Digital Ship White Paper* (COSMO Shipping and China Classification Society) pushes for connecting data across ships, ports, cargo, and people; an industry-level digital foundation is a prerequisite no single enterprise can build alone.

## 10.8 Healthcare

### 10.8.1 Industry Profile and Bottleneck Triage

Healthcare is a typical information-physical hybrid: diagnosis, medical records, and imaging interpretation are essentially information processing, but treatment outcomes ultimately land in physical actions such as surgery, medication, and nursing. Its "information-to-physical bottleneck" ratio sits around 30–50:50–70 — which means healthcare AI cannot rewrite entire processes digitally the way finance can, and must embed itself in an "augmenting people" rather than "replacing people" mode. Grand View Research forecasts the global healthcare AI market growing from US$36.7 billion in 2025 to US$505.6 billion by 2033, and EO Intelligence (Chinese: 亿欧智库) estimates China's healthcare LLM market exceeded RMB 1.1 billion in 2025 [third-party verified].

But the growth narrative masks four structural contradictions. First, regulatory compliance: the FDA has approved over a thousand AI/ML medical devices, yet no generative AI has received FDA approval; in China, Class III medical device registration certificates are the precondition for AI to enter clinical billing, and the number of certificates directly constitutes a corporate moat (United Imaging 16, Deepwise (Chinese: 深睿) and Shukun Technology (Chinese: 数坤) 14 each, Infervision (Chinese: 推想) 13) [third-party verified]. Second, clinical accountability: responsibility allocation after AI enters high-risk operations is far from mature — US surgical robots caused 1,391 incidents and 144 patient deaths over 13 years [third-party verified]. Third, data privacy: the WHO warned in November 2025 of a serious gap between medical AI expansion and legal-ethical safeguards, noting that biased training data will amplify healthcare inequities [third-party verified]. Fourth, physician workflow integration: AI alone can reach 90% diagnostic accuracy, yet physician use of assistance improves accuracy by only about 2 percentage points [third-party verified] — human-AI collaboration gains depend on workflow design and trust building, not model accuracy.

### 10.8.2 Transformation Paths and Typical Practices

Three paths diverge clearly.

**Chinese hospitals: open-source models + local deployment + hospital-wide infrastructure.** DeepSeek's open-sourcing in early 2025 dramatically lowered the AI threshold for hospitals: Shenzhen Nanshan Hospital deployed DeepSeek-R1 671B locally in 72 hours to build three application scenarios; the University of Hong Kong-Shenzhen Hospital deployed R1 under a "data never leaves the hospital" model; Peking University Third Hospital used multiple combined deployment modes to cover the entire hospital [third-party verified]. Scenarios are spreading quickly: West China Hospital (Chinese: 华西医院), based on its "Hongyi" LLM (Chinese: 华西黉医), released two agents — patient accompaniment and medical documentation — and the documentation agent has generated nearly 600,000 medical records since launch [third-party verified]; the first publicized 149 granular hospital AI scenarios show AI penetrating the full flow of appointment triage, medical-insurance cost control, DRG grouping, and medication review. On the budget side, the industry has entered an "infrastructure era": Zhongshan Hospital's (Chinese: 中山医院) RMB 160 million AI platform tender was awarded; iFlytek (Chinese: 科大讯飞), Alipay, and Baidu successively won government healthcare procurement contracts exceeding RMB 100 million; and hospital AI budgets jumped from millions to hundreds of millions of RMB [third-party verified]. IDC estimates China's AI + healthcare application software market at RMB 3.54 billion in 2025, projected to reach RMB 14 billion by 2030, and judges that the watershed has shifted from "how strong is the model" to "how deeply can business flows be redesigned" [third-party verified].

**US hospitals: administration first, clinical caution, portfolio governance.** HCA Healthcare (191 hospitals) advances in layers: on the clinical side, it partners with Google to optimize nurse handoffs, and its fetal heart monitoring AI has been submitted for FDA review; on the operations side, nearly 100 hospitals have deployed scheduling and workforce tools; the administrative side is seen as the "shortest path to value." Its CFO admitted that "rolling out AI and changing frontline behavior is far harder than imagined," and investment focus is shifting toward change management [third-party verified]. Mayo Clinic will invest over US$1 billion over the next few years across 200+ projects, driven by the dual engines of "clinical research + AI engineering"; Advocate Health uses an "evaluation funnel" (225 proposals → 40 use cases) for centralized selection — administration first, clinical later [third-party verified]. Menlo Ventures surveyed 700+ US healthcare executives: the industry deploys AI at 2.2x the pace of other industries, 22% of organizations have deployed domain-specific tools (up 7x year over year), and 2025 healthcare AI spending was about US$1.4 billion, roughly 3x the prior year [third-party verified].

**AI drug discovery and regulation: paradigm shift with institutions first.** Insilico Medicine (Chinese: 英矽智能) applies generative AI across the full pipeline from target discovery to clinical development, closing more than 13 BD deals in 2025 with cumulative potential value exceeding US$10 billion — the first profitable results for AI drug discovery [third-party verified]; its CEO claims AI compresses the drug R&D cycle to about one year (candidate drugs in as little as 9 months vs roughly 4.5 years traditionally) [official claims; note: "candidate drug" ≠ "approved new drug"]. On the regulatory side, the FDA published its final guidance on PCCP (Predetermined Change Control Plans) in August 2025, allowing AI algorithms to iterate continuously within predetermined bounds without re-filing — this "learning license" regime raises the organizational bar for continuous monitoring and change management; Fudan University, Ruijin Hospital (Chinese: 瑞金医院), and Ant Group (Chinese: 蚂蚁集团) jointly released the *Evaluation Standards for Healthcare Large Models*, providing a common language for "who evaluates, and how" [third-party verified].

### 10.8.3 Benchmark Cases

**Kaiser Permanente: Ambient Documentation Across 7,260 Physicians [third-party verified, NEJM Catalyst].** From October 2023 to December 2024, The Permanente Medical Group (TPMG) deployed ambient AI documentation at 17 medical centers in Northern California, covering 7,260 physicians and over 2 million visits. Physicians collectively saved 15,700 hours of documentation time, and after-hours documentation time fell by an average of 1.03 minutes per visit; 84% of surveyed physicians said AI improved their connection with patients, and 82% reported higher job satisfaction. Combined with Abridge's largest generative deployment across 40 Kaiser hospitals and 600+ clinics, ambient documentation has become the most commercially mature track in healthcare AI. But the Peterson Health Technology Institute cautions: financial returns of the systems have not yet been validated at scale, and scaling depends on deep EHR integration and safety monitoring systems.

**Zhiyi Assistant (Chinese: 智医助理): 1.2 Billion Assisted Diagnoses Reaching the Grassroots [third-party verified / official disclosures].** iFlytek's "Zhiyi Assistant" embeds "AI-assisted diagnosis + standardized electronic medical records" into grassroots workflows: by the end of 2025 it covered 31 provinces, 800+ counties and districts, and 77,000+ grassroots medical institutions, with over 1.2 billion assisted diagnoses cumulatively serving 250,000+ grassroots physicians [third-party reported figure]; Liu Qingfeng disclosed that in Anhui alone it corrected 170,000 erroneous diagnoses in a single year [official claims]. Its organizational form — "provincial coordination, government funding, embedded systems" — validates the grassroots accessibility path while also exposing dependence on data quality and government funding.

**Beijing Anzhen Hospital (Chinese: 安贞医院): The "Report Revolution" of Operations-Management AI [third-party verified].** Released in March 2026 as the country's first "public hospital operations management LLM," it was developed with China Telecom over 7 months, integrating the HIS, SPD, and ODR systems, defining 200+ data standards, governing 3 million+ data records, and establishing 800+ core management indicators. Operations-management report generation was cut from days to 5 minutes, management response speed improved by 95%, and precision decision-making efficiency improved by 60%; the "data-query agent + operations analysis engine" supports multi-level drill-down attribution and delivers role-personalized decision reports — "a thousand people, a thousand faces." It represents hospital AI extending from clinical assistance to operational decision-making, rebuilding management's data-decision infrastructure.

### 10.8.4 Failure Warnings

**HIMSS 2025: 80% of Projects Fail to Convert Value [third-party verified].** A HIMSS panel confronted the industry's pain point directly: 80% of healthcare AI projects fail to convert into value; 97% of organizations agree on deployment urgency, but only 14% have effective implementation capability. Its verdict cuts to the heart of the matter: "AI failure is not technical immaturity but strategic failure," and the biggest mistake is trying to solve all problems at once.

**NHS: The Cold Landing of a National Program [third-party verified].** UCL, with the Nuffield Trust, evaluated NHS England's £21 million AI chest-imaging project across 66 hospital trusts: contract signing was delayed 4–10 months, and 18 months after contract completion, 23 of 66 trusts (a third) still had not used it clinically. The barrier is not the algorithm but the organization: overworked clinicians unable to participate, aging and heterogeneous IT systems difficult to integrate, and senior physicians worried about accountability for AI decisions.

**Hallucinations and the Clinical Adoption Gap [third-party verified].** Healthcare LLMs inherently carry hallucination risk; the industry consensus is that it "cannot be fully eliminated, only managed and corrected." AI alone achieves 90% diagnostic accuracy while physician assistance improves accuracy by only about 2 percentage points — without workflow design and trust building, accuracy does not automatically translate into outcomes. The 1,391 incidents and 144 deaths from surgical robots over 13 years is a reminder that AI adoption in high-risk clinical operations advances far more slowly than in administrative settings, and accountability, standards, and insurance mechanisms are far from mature. An HFMA survey shows nearly 70% of CFOs admit governance maturity lags adoption speed, and resource misallocation of "bought but unused, used but unevaluated, evaluated but not retired" is happening [third-party verified].

### 10.8.5 Organizational Patterns and Consulting Takeaways

First, **the organizational loop of "AI alert → human confirmation → intervention" is the value multiplier of clinical AI.** UCSD's COMPOSER reduced in-hospital mortality by 17%, and community hospital Augusta Health saved 282 lives in two years through the same path — the common factor is not a stronger model but embedding alerts into care workflows, training response mechanisms, and continuously recalibrating the model. For small and medium hospitals, the lesson: start with a single high-value scenario and compensate for resource disadvantages with organizational mechanisms.

Second, **change management is the main bottleneck, not technology.** A McKinsey survey shows 50% of healthcare organizations have implemented AI, yet its conclusion is that "the main bottleneck is not technology but leadership and organizational change," corroborating the NHS research; HCA's CFO admitted that changing frontline behavior is far harder than deploying tools.

Third, **physician-adoption design sets the ceiling.** Kaiser's high satisfaction comes from embedding AI into existing workflows rather than adding burden; HIMSS stresses that "unexplainable AI decisions will lose physician-patient trust." Consulting takeaways: do workflow analysis before model selection, and build trust administration-first, clinical-later.

Fourth, **regulation-first is a roadmap, not a barrier.** Class III certificates, FDA approvals, algorithm registration, and data security assessments form a landscape where "admission barriers are organizational capabilities": certificate counts become corporate moats, buyers need new validation and acceptance capabilities, and third-party evaluation standards are becoming a common language for all parties.

Fifth, **data assets replace models as the biggest shortfall.** The consensus at the VB100 conference: the biggest shortfall is no longer compute or models but standardized, high-quality data assets; IDC judges the next five years' competition will hinge on "how deeply business flows can be redesigned." The essence of AI-native transformation in healthcare is reordering four things simultaneously — clinical workflows, management decisions, regulatory compliance, and data governance. Technically, the hardest part is keeping AI from overstepping before the physician signs off; organizationally, the hardest part is getting AI actually used after sign-off.

## 10.9 Manufacturing

### 10.9.1 Industry Profile and Bottleneck Triage

Among all industries in this book, manufacturing has the highest physical-bottleneck share: the information-to-physical bottleneck ratio is roughly 20–40:60–80. No matter how smart the algorithms, everything ultimately submits to the physical realities of machine-tool cycle times, material flow, and equipment wear. Yet it is precisely this least "digital" industry that McKinsey identifies as the largest generative-AI value pool — capable of delivering up to 2x productivity gains and nearly 70% task automation for manufacturing and supply chains, with nearly a quarter of China's ~US$2 trillion generative-AI value coming from manufacturing and supply chains.

Enormous value alongside dismal deployment — the gap constitutes three core contradictions. First, the mismatch between production-line physical constraints and data intelligence: AI accelerates the decision layer, not the physical layer; cycle time, precision, and process stability cannot be accelerated directly by models. Second, industrial data is inherently heterogeneous: proprietary device protocols, SCADA/ERP/MES systems each in their own silos, and defect samples scarce to an extreme — hundreds of thousands of good-part images versus just twenty-some defective-part images, with labeling still depending on veteran workers about to retire. Third, ROI is hard to verify: a Deloitte survey shows 91% of industrial AI projects fall short of expectations, and 90% of failures are not technical.

### 10.9.2 Transformation Paths and Typical Practices

**Lighthouse factories: a standardized paradigm from "points" to "surfaces."** China leads the world in lighthouse factory count — Haier (Chinese: 海尔) with 13 cumulative sites and Foxconn (Chinese: 富士康) with 5 plants in the network. McKinsey data shows nearly 60% of use cases at newly designated lighthouses already leverage AI, with productivity up 2–3x, defect rates down 99%, and energy consumption improved by 30%. The real value of lighthouse factories lies not in technology but in organizational methodology: Foxconn turns its experience into white papers and extends toward "Lighthouse Industry," while Haier empowers externally through COSMOPlat (Chinese: 卡奥斯).

**Industrial LLMs: from general chat to industry "operating systems."** Huawei predicts industrial LLMs will become manufacturing's "operating system," with penetration expected to reach 50% in China by 2030 and market size to exceed RMB 42 billion. Players split into the big tech (Baidu, Huawei) and the "industry columns" (COSMOPlat's "Tianzhi," the Digital & Intelligent Building Materials Research Institute's (Chinese: 数智建材研究院) "Xiaomiao," Zhonggong Hulian's (Chinese: 中工互联) "Zhigong"). Shandong Province's LLM industry revenue reached RMB 6.38 billion in 2025, up over 70% year on year, organized through open-competition challenges.

**Industrial agents: from "usable" to "affordable and replicable."** CCID Consulting (Chinese: 赛迪顾问) data shows China's agent market at RMB 7.84 billion in 2025, projected to reach RMB 13.53 billion in 2026. 2025 has been called the "first year of industrial agents," with deployment mostly still at the demonstration stage; 2026 enters the "replication era," and the challenge shifts from technology to engineering. Vendors such as Advantech (Chinese: 研华) embed agents into existing production-line systems.

**AI quality inspection and predictive maintenance: the two single-point scenarios with the clearest ROI.** AI visual inspection is the fastest-penetrating scenario (China's smart inspection output value reached RMB 574.5 billion in 2025); vendors claim it can cut miss rates to 1% of manual inspection and save 70% of re-inspection costs [official claims]. IBM, citing ISA data, says plants typically lose 5%–20% of manufacturing capacity to downtime, and AI can cut unplanned downtime by 30%–50% [vendor claims] — but deployment pain points are prominent: legacy equipment without sensors, scarce failure samples, and ROI hard to quantify.

**Platform transformation: manufacturers incubating AI platforms.** SANY (Chinese: 三一) partners with RootCloud (Chinese: 树根互联), Haier incubates COSMOPlat, and Midea (Chinese: 美的) productizes its "Meiqing" (Chinese: 美擎) experience for external clients — forming a division of labor where "big companies build platforms, SMEs get on platforms." At the institutional level, the eight-department *Implementation Plan for the "AI + Manufacturing" Special Action* led by MIIT, combined with guidelines covering 8 links and 40 scenarios plus tiered cultivation, constitutes a tripartite promotion framework.

### 10.9.3 Benchmark Cases

**Shenglong Electric (Chinese: 盛隆电气): 27 Agents Deployed in 14 Days [third-party verified]**

The *2026 White Paper on AI Scenario Applications in Chinese Manufacturing* documents 167 deployment cases with "named enterprises and quantifiable data," among which Shenglong Electric deployed 27 industrial agents in 14 days, cutting labor coordination costs by 60% with ROI over 200% [third-party verified]. The white paper also offers sober structural data: generative AI accounts for only 4.8% of applications and AI agents only 3%, while traditional machine learning (46.7%) and machine vision (20.4%) remain the mainstays — "existing technology is sufficient; the bottleneck is scenario matching and the deployment approach."

**Figure × BMW: Humanoid Robots Enter a Production Line [official claims]**

Figure 02 completed an 11-month deployment at BMW's Spartanburg plant: over 1,250 hours of operation, 90,000+ components loaded, and contributions to the production of 30,000+ X3 vehicles; sheet-metal loading completed within an 84-second cycle at 5 mm tolerance, with a positioning success rate over 99% [official claims]. BMW's Munich plant, by contrast, represents the "digital-native" rebuild route: digital twins run across development and production validation, and a partnership with Siemens and NVIDIA accelerated pneumatic simulation 30x [third-party verified]. Embodied intelligence has obtained production-scale data for the first time, validating its engineering boundaries.

**CITIC Pacific Special Steel (Chinese: 中信泰富特钢): Industrial AI for Process Industries [third-party verified]**

In McKinsey's lighthouse factory cases, CITIC Pacific Special Steel uses AI to precisely predict and adjust blast furnace operations in real time, raising output by 15% and cutting energy consumption by 11% [third-party verified]. This represents the typical form of process-industry AI: fusing "mechanism models + data models" to optimize process parameters rather than replace people — chemicals likewise concentrates on four directions (process optimization, predictive maintenance, safety early warning, and energy optimization), and must be led by the process technology department.

### 10.9.4 Failure Warnings

**91% Fall Short of Expectations: The Failure Is Organizational and Data-Related, Not Technical [third-party verified]**

A Deloitte survey shows only 9% of Chinese manufacturing AI projects meet 80%–100% of expectations. The failure attributions are strikingly consistent: requirements get distorted after passing through three layers — "decision-makers → IT department → vendor" (one company invested millions in a "knowledge assistant"; daily active users fell to single digits within three months of launch and the project was halted); frontline workers did not trust a 99%-accurate system and insisted on manual re-inspection; and after vendor delivery, nobody maintained the model, the system degraded, and it was shut down. The common traits of surviving projects: entry through small scenarios, worker participation in requirements definition, and data governance front-loaded.

**The AI Fabric Inspector: RMB 3.7 Million Invested, 15% Misjudgment Rate [third-party verified]**

A textile company in the Yangtze River Delta invested RMB 3.7 million in an AI fabric inspection system, but fabric reflection issues drove the actual misjudgment rate as high as 15%; the company ultimately kept manual re-inspection and relegated AI to an auxiliary role. The lesson: insufficient scenario diversity and standardization, samples disconnected from process know-how, and single-point equipment retrofits cannot solve systemic problems.

**Rushing into AI Quality Inspection: A Missing Data Foundation [third-party verified]**

A manufacturer rushed full-process AI quality inspection without pilots or data preparation; insufficient training data led to subpar accuracy and production lines that could not run, and the project failed and had to be redone — about 60% of enterprises ignore that "data and business preparation precede technology." The common thread of failure is not weak algorithms but organizational and data infrastructure lagging behind.

### 10.9.5 Organizational Patterns and Consulting Takeaways

First, **recognize AI's boundaries in a physically bottlenecked industry.** Manufacturing AI's ROI concentrates in the decision layer and the "documentation and administrative layer around production" (SOP assistants, reports, knowledge Q&A, production scheduling), not in the physical layer itself — in McKinsey's lighthouse analysis, the fastest generative-AI gains appear in process and knowledge scenarios outside the shop floor (ACG Capsules' SOP assistant was used by nearly three-quarters of operators within five weeks). The first consulting move is to distinguish "physical cycle-time problems" (which AI cannot manage) from "decision and information problems" (which AI can).

Second, **single point first, then platform, then organization.** Start with high-frequency single points such as inspection and production scheduling to validate ROI, then build a data platform and model asset management, and finally change the organization (Chief AI Officer, AI Center of Excellence, business-unit AI specialists). McKinsey offers three scaling levers: packaging use cases as "assets," accelerating with low-code platforms, and phased introduction — AI assists decisions first, and only automates after confidence thresholds are exceeded.

Third, **the data foundation sets AI's ceiling.** Defect-sample generation, OT/IT integration, and the fusion of mechanism and data models are the three engineering prerequisites; predictive maintenance — the clearest-ROI scenario — is precisely where "data infrastructure debt" is most fully exposed.

Fourth, **manufacturers incubating AI platforms have three organizational paths:** internal incubation + external independent operation (SANY/RootCloud); parent-factory validation + platform company external delivery (Haier COSMOPlat, Midea Meiqing); and dual entities of "research institute + industrial company" (the Digital & Intelligent Building Materials Research Institute/"Xiaomiao"). The common thread is detaching AI capability from the parent's organizational inertia and competing in the market as independent entities.

Fifth, **business-led, not IT-led.** The chemicals industry explicitly requires leadership "by the process technology department rather than the IT department"; academic research summarizes manufacturing AI transformation as a staged leap of "cognitive awakening → strategic decision → organizational change → technology deployment → capability consolidation" — AI-native transformation is never a technology procurement problem, but a "cognition-behavior" double-helix organizational learning process.

## 10.10 Energy & Utilities

### 10.10.1 Industry Profile and Bottleneck Triage

Among the industries examined in this book, energy and utilities has the highest physical-bottleneck share: the information-to-physical bottleneck ratio is roughly 20–30:70–80. Grid power flows, pipeline pressures, and turbine speeds are all millisecond-level physical realities — no matter how smart the algorithms, Joule's law cannot be changed. The global energy AI market exceeded US$40 billion in 2025, and the power industry's AI agent market exceeded RMB 40 billion [third-party verified], yet value has been slow to materialize — utilities invested over US$10 billion in AI over the past five years, with most projects never progressing beyond the pilot stage [third-party verified].

Behind the gap are four core contradictions. First, physical safety constraints are the top priority: dispatch errors can trigger large-scale blackouts or even casualties; the US NERC CIP regulatory framework has no clear definition for AI models; and the nuclear industry requires AI to enter only "human-machine collaboration" scenarios, never autonomous control. Second, asset-heavy and long-cycle: legacy systems 20–30 years old — SCADA, mainframes — cannot communicate with modern AI platforms, and companies spend 40% of their AI budgets making old systems "talk" [third-party verified]. Third, data fragmentation: data is scattered across dozens of isolated systems, and models are trained on incomplete, biased datasets [third-party verified]. Fourth, the dual constraints of regulation and performance assessment: central SOEs face assessments and grids must guarantee supply, leaving far less room for error than the internet industry.

### 10.10.2 Transformation Paths and Typical Practices

**Grid AI: industry LLMs become the new "foundation."** State Grid (Chinese: 国家电网) released China's first 100-billion-parameter multimodal power LLM, "Guangming" (Chinese: 光明), in December 2024, and its "6541" master plan in May 2025 (six application domains, five intelligent systems, and a four-in-one foundation); its intelligent customer service now auto-responds to over 90% of work orders, serving 270 million interactions cumulatively [official claims]. China Southern Power Grid (Chinese: 南方电网) takes the domestic-compute route: "DaWatt · Yudian" (Chinese: 大瓦特·驭电), trained on Huawei Ascend clusters with 13 billion parameters, was selected among the ten "national heavyweights" of central SOEs, and platform model calls exceeded 10 billion in December 2025 [official claims]. Both grid giants use a "headquarters LLM + localized small models" architecture: a dispatch robot in Xuancheng, Anhui compressed dispatch-instruction drafting from 3 hours to 30 minutes, and converter-transformer fault diagnosis in Liaocheng, Shandong was shortened from a week to milliseconds [third-party verified].

**Oil & gas LLMs: from single-well intelligence to an "industry brain."** CNPC's (Chinese: 中国石油) Kunlun LLM (Chinese: 昆仑大模型) released a 300-billion-parameter version (MoE architecture) in May 2025, covering 26 business lines, 119 business domains, and 100 core scenarios [official claims]; Sinopec (Chinese: 中石化) uses the "Great Wall" LLM (Chinese: 长城大模型) as its group foundation. Data has been elevated to equal standing with models: CNPC built a 550TB three-tier data management system, and "data is the blood of model training" is becoming industry consensus [third-party verified].

**Renewable operations: AI from "veteran experience" to "precision diagnosis."** Wind and solar installed capacity reached 1.482 billion kW in Q1 2025, surpassing thermal power for the first time, and the surge in installations is pushing operations and maintenance toward AI. Envision Energy (Chinese: 远景) trains agents on millions of real inspection records, letting AI "understand the language of wind turbines" [third-party verified]; Goldwind (Chinese: 金风) uses digital twins + IoT for predictive maintenance [third-party verified]; Shenzhen Energy (Chinese: 深圳能源), with Huawei Cloud, released a power forecasting system to reduce wind and solar curtailment [official claims]. The industry's overall fault-identification accuracy has been raised to over 90% [third-party verified].

**Nuclear power and virtual power plants: the two endpoints of most conservative and most cutting-edge.** In nuclear power, CNNC Nuclear Power's (Chinese: 中国核电) "Hezhi AI Portal" (Chinese: 核智 AI 门户) integrates DeepSeek, and CGN (Chinese: 中广核) has built a nuclear full-lifecycle intelligence hub on 18 core datasets — but applications are strictly limited to "human-machine collaboration" scenarios such as decision support and procedure interpretation [third-party verified]. In virtual power plants, an energy storage station in Dongguan completes 10MW power allocation in 0.5 seconds via AI dispatch [third-party verified]; State Grid Shanghai plans a "4+4" set of eight agents, and State Grid Hangzhou proposes a resource-layer—aggregation-layer—market-layer framework [third-party verified]; in 2025 China's VPP regulation capacity targets 20 million kW, with the market exceeding RMB 100 billion [third-party verified].

### 10.10.3 Benchmark Cases

**Changqing Oilfield × Huawei: A Fully Autonomous Smart Oilfield [official claims]**

Changqing (Chinese: 长庆油田), China's largest onshore oil and gas field (annual oil and gas equivalent output of over 60 million tonnes, managing 126,000 wells), partnered with Huawei and Kunlun Digital (Chinese: 昆仑数智) to build a three-tier architecture of "cloud-centralized training — regional inference management — edge real-time inference" on a fully autonomous technology stack of "Kunpeng + Ascend + Galaxy Kylin." The "intelligent hazard identification for special operations" project collected 2 million operation images and 150,000 hours of video, built a knowledge base of 12,000 rules, and deployed 20 high-precision models: hazard identification accuracy of 94.1% (18.7 percentage points over traditional methods), edge inference latency under 800 ms, manual supervision intervention in high-risk operations down 70%, and overall operations and maintenance costs down 18%; reservoir development detection accuracy improved from 78% to 92%, and unplanned equipment downtime fell by 50% [official claims].

**Duke Energy: Predictive Maintenance Proven at Scale on the Distribution Grid [third-party verified]**

In 2025, Duke Energy's AI grid prevented about 1.2 million outages in the Carolinas — resolving outages before they reached customers and saving about 3 million hours of customer outage time cumulatively [third-party verified]. The approach is not flashy: AI prediction models identify equipment failure risk and vegetation hazards, prioritize risk using weather and asset condition, and schedule trimming and maintenance in advance. Even more valuable is the organizational method: starting in 2017 with a single electricity-theft detection use case, governance guardrails were established in 2024, and by 2025 over 50 generative-AI use cases were in flight. The CIO's three principles — "Always on" (reliability first), "Data before dazzle" (don't plug AI into bad data), and "Human in the loop" (AI augments people rather than replacing them) — are a classic articulation of utility AI governance [third-party verified].

**The Kunlun LLM: An Organizational Sample of an SOE "Industry Brain" [official claims]**

The Kunlun LLM was launched by the group's Party leadership in January 2024, became the first LLM in the energy and chemicals industry to pass national filing in August 2024, and raised its parameter count to 300 billion in May 2025 [official claims]. It represents a collaboration model of "central-SOE headquarters coordination + ecosystem partner co-building," with the goal of upgrading from "model capability" to a reusable "industry brain" for the whole sector [third-party verified].

### 10.10.4 Failure Warnings

**74% of Pilots Never Leave a Single Region [third-party verified].** Prudent Consulting research shows that 74% of utility AI pilots never reach production beyond a single region. The six root causes are nearly all organizational: legacy system integration barriers (40% of budget spent on integrating old systems), data architecture not designed for AI, no AI lifecycle management (models abandoned as accuracy decays without retraining for 12–18 months), skill gaps and organizational misalignment, missing governance, and improper ROI measurement [third-party verified].

**70% of Leaders Are Dissatisfied with AI Progress [third-party verified].** A BCG 2024 survey shows nearly 60% of energy company leaders expect AI to deliver within a year, but about 70% are dissatisfied with progress — the expectation-reality gap constitutes the energy industry's "AI anxiety" [third-party verified]. The industry also widely suffers from "built but unused": projects completed without operational talent, models reduced to decoration [third-party verified].

**Common Threads Behind Pilots That Fail to Scale:** model decay, data silos, and the disconnect between pilot and production systems. The IEA also notes the energy industry broadly underutilizes AI, with data availability and digital skills gaps as the main barriers [third-party verified]. Accenture's *2025 China Enterprise Digital Transformation Index* likewise shows only about 9% of enterprises capture significant value from generative-AI implementation [third-party verified].

### 10.10.5 Organizational Patterns and Consulting Takeaways

First, **define AI's physical safety boundary first.** Energy is an industry where "errors are irreversible": AI's value concentrates in the decision and information layer — inspection recognition, load forecasting, dispatch assistance, and knowledge retrieval — and wherever people and grid safety are involved, human accountability must remain. The early lesson from the US GridFWD conference is that "capital is too precious and the risk of being wrong is too great" — build governance models before talking about use cases [third-party verified]. The first consulting move is to help enterprises distinguish scenarios where AI may only "suggest" from those where it may "execute."

Second, **single point first, then system, then organization.** Duke started with one electricity-theft detection use case in 2017, built governance guardrails in 2024, and only scaled in 2025; State Grid Hangzhou's experience is to prioritize "high-frequency, high-value, low-risk" scenarios and to set up cross-department AI teams [third-party verified]. "Slow first, then fast — earn the right to move quickly" is closer to the truth than rushing in [third-party verified].

Third, **the data foundation and centralized control centers are dual prerequisites.** Prudent lists "data architecture not designed for AI" as a root cause [third-party verified]; Changqing invested in 2 million images + 150,000 hours of video + a 12,000-rule knowledge base [official claims], and State Grid trained the Guangming LLM on 20 years of operational databases + 5 million work orders [official claims]. Central SOEs generally adopt a "group-level platform + edge inference" centralized control architecture, treating data governance and compute infrastructure as infrastructure rather than projects.

Fourth, **the peculiarities of SOE organizational change: assessment and fault tolerance.** State Grid moved from the "6541 plan" to a special action of "two years to consolidate the foundation, three years to drive improvement" [official claims], and China Southern Power Grid deploys 29 key initiatives through its annual work plan [third-party verified] — AI is upgraded from project-based to routine mechanisms, driven by documents, assessments, and dedicated bodies. But assessment-driven progress has its cost: the "built but unused" phenomenon is prominent. The consulting takeaway is to design a dual-track mechanism of "pilot fault tolerance + scale-up assessment," so AI does not become ledger-based political achievement.

Fifth, **upgrade AI from a tool to strategy and business model.** BCG advocates moving from "piloting with AI" to an "AI-first operating model": AI embedded in core business processes rather than edge applications, fused teams of data scientists and domain experts, and co-development with technology companies rather than procurement [third-party verified]. Guidehouse goes further: AI will push utilities from "operational optimization" to "business model reconstruction" — virtual power plants and dynamic pricing become possible [third-party verified]. And the IEA's proposition that "without AI there is no energy, and without energy there is no AI" [third-party verified] makes energy the only industry where "AI demand creators and users are one" — the biggest strategic variable for AI-native energy organizations.
